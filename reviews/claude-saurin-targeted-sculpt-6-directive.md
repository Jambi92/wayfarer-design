# Claude Directive — Saurin Targeted Sculpt 6: Structural Plane & Ridge Architecture

**Date:** October 1, 2026  
**From:** ChatGPT (Author), incorporating Tyler's direct visual review of TS5  
**Status:** REQUIRED REVISION  
**Scope:** Underlying Saurin anatomical form language—head first, body second. Preserve successful TS5 work. No UE5 implementation.

## 1. Tyler's visual diagnosis

TS5 is another meaningful improvement. The cranial keratin display concept works, the tail phenotype exploration works, and the Saurin is moving farther away from a human morph.

However, Tyler identified the remaining visual problem very clearly:

> **The Saurin still feels too rounded. Reptilian anatomy often reads through sudden, sharp changes of plane, ridges, edges and structural transitions. The Saurin needs more of that language.**

This is now the central objective of TS6.

The problem is **not** insufficient scale texture. It is **not** insufficient horns. It is **not** solved by adding random spikes.

The underlying anatomy itself is still dominated by soft, inflated, continuously rounded forms. In several regions, the result can still read like mammalian/human volumes covered with reptile texture.

### Governing instruction

> **Stop treating the Saurin as rounded volumes covered in reptile texture. Build a reptilian skeletal landscape of ridges, planes, edges, recesses and controlled transitions, then allow scales and keratin display structures to follow that anatomy.**

## 2. Reference study — prioritize skeletal structure

For TS6, examine real reptile **bare skull geometry and skeletal anatomy**, not only photographs of living skin/scales.

Study multiple families for principles, not copying:
- varanid/monitor skulls;
- iguanid and agamid skulls;
- crocodilian skull/jaw construction;
- other lizard skulls with clear orbital, temporal, rostral and jaw-plane changes;
- reptilian limb girdles and long-bone/joint transitions where useful.

Identify which visible changes come from:
1. bone;
2. muscular/soft-tissue attachment;
3. scale fields;
4. keratin display structures.

The Saurin design must deliberately separate these layers.

Do not reproduce one real species. Saurin remain a distinct fantasy humanoid lineage.

## 3. New positive structural concept

Add a Saurin anatomical concept provisionally called:

# **Craniofacial Ridge-and-Plane Architecture**

This is **underlying anatomy**, distinct from the optional Cranial Keratin Display System.

Definition:

> **Saurin craniofacial anatomy is organized through deliberate plane changes and structurally integrated ridges around the orbital platform, rostrum, temporal region, jaw and posterior skull. Broad surfaces may remain relatively smooth, but transitions between major anatomical regions are comparatively decisive rather than continuously rounded. This creates a recognizable reptilian skeletal landscape even when scales, pigmentation, pupils and optional keratin display structures are removed.**

This architecture should be present in all normal adult Saurin, with individual variation in strength and exact expression.

It must not create a permanent angry expression.

## 4. Critical distinction: structural ridges vs display structures

Do not confuse these systems.

### Structural ridge/plane anatomy
- part of skull/body form;
- remains on minimal-display Saurin;
- establishes anatomical region boundaries;
- supports muscle/soft tissue;
- cannot simply be toggled off like a hairstyle.

### Cranial Keratin Display System
- horns;
- hornlets;
- crests;
- spikes;
- keratin plates;
- variable individual expression;
- hair-equivalent identity/customization role.

A Saurin with **minimal cranial display** must still look unmistakably reptilian because the skull underneath already has the correct architecture.

## 5. Head — orbital architecture

The orbit currently needs more structural definition.

Develop:
- stronger supraorbital structural rim/plane;
- clear lateral/postorbital transition;
- defined relationship between orbital platform and temporal region;
- controlled recess around the eye socket;
- clear transition from orbit into rostral root.

Do not simply add an "angry eyebrow."

The orbital rim should be anatomical rather than expressive.

Neutral Saurin must still be capable of friendly, calm, frightened, curious and other expressions later.

Preserve:
- forward-readable gaze;
- vertical pupils;
- current improved eye seating;
- adult proportions.

## 6. Head — rostral architecture

The rostrum should no longer read as one smooth tapered cone/wedge.

Build a more deliberate set of surfaces:
- dorsal rostral plane;
- paired or bilateral longitudinal structural transitions where appropriate;
- lateral rostral planes;
- ventrolateral/maxillary transition;
- anterior terminal plane;
- coherent nostril-bearing region.

Possible paired dorsal ridges may be explored, but they must emerge from skull construction rather than look pasted on.

The rostrum should remain compact enough to preserve approved Saurin proportions. Do not turn it into a crocodile snout.

The anterior tip must continue moving away from the old round mammalian nose-pad read.

## 7. Head — cheek/maxillary/temporal transitions

This is a major opportunity.

Currently, several lateral surfaces blend together too softly.

Establish readable transitions between:
- rostral base;
- maxillary/cheek region;
- orbital platform;
- postorbital region;
- temporal region;
- posterior skull;
- jaw hinge.

These transitions do not all need knife-sharp edges. Some can be broad ridges or changes of curvature.

The goal is a **hierarchy of planes**, not a faceted low-poly appearance.

## 8. Head — mandible

Continue TS4/TS5 jaw improvements.

The mandible should gain:
- clearer inferior/lateral plane;
- stronger posterior structural turn;
- readable ramus/hinge;
- controlled angularity where mandibular body transitions toward the posterior jaw;
- better relationship to temporal musculature and throat.

Avoid:
- human chin;
- huge monster jaw;
- permanent grin;
- crocodilian copy;
- razor-thin jaw edge.

The mouth remains the boundary created by upper and lower jaw anatomy, not a line sculpted around a muzzle.

## 9. Head — posterior skull and occipital transition

The rear cranium is still relatively bulbous/rounded.

Explore:
- stronger temporal plane;
- clearer posterior cranial transition;
- restrained occipital ridge/plane logic;
- better skull-to-neck structural integration.

Do not flatten the skull into a box. We want organic but decisive structure.

## 10. Head — front-view priority

The Saurin has repeatedly looked stronger in profile than straight-on.

TS6 must specifically solve the **front view**.

From the front, we should read:
- orbital structural architecture;
- rostral root;
- lateral rostral planes;
- anterior rostral termination;
- upper/lower jaw relationship;
- cheek/maxillary transition;
- bilateral skull structure.

It must not collapse back into:
**eyes + round nose + horizontal mouth**.

This front-view requirement is a major acceptance criterion.

## 11. Body extension — controlled structural angularity

After the head works, carry a **subtler version** of this design language into the body.

Do not turn the Saurin into an armored dinosaur.

Potential regions:
- neck-to-shoulder transition;
- clavicular/upper thoracic region;
- scapular region;
- elbow;
- forearm;
- wrist;
- pelvis/sacral transition;
- knee;
- shin;
- ankle;
- tail base;
- dorsal tail.

The body should retain organic soft tissue and composition variation. Structural transitions should appear where anatomy supports them.

Avoid simply sharpening every muscle.

## 12. Tail structural language

The TS5 tail-variation concept is successful and should be preserved.

Improve tail anatomy by exploring controlled structural definition:
- clearer sacral-to-caudal transition;
- dorsal caudal line/ridge where appropriate;
- lateral/ventral plane differences;
- scale fields following underlying tail geometry;
- optional keratin display elements remaining separate from baseline structural anatomy.

Do not make every tail spiky.

The six TS5 tail phenotype directions remain valid exploratory examples:
- balanced;
- shorter/heavier;
- longer/gradual taper;
- laterally broad/proximal mass;
- restrained dorsal keratin;
- slender/inherited banding.

## 13. Hands and feet

Preserve the accepted direction:
- tapered distal phalanges;
- modest pointed claws;
- claws continue digit axes;
- functional grasp;
- plantigrade foot;
- non-human forefoot/toe read.

If body structural angularity is extended into the limbs, do it through believable joint/shaft transitions—not by making fingers/toes blade-like.

## 14. Scale architecture must follow form

Regional Scale Architecture remains canonical.

TS6 should demonstrate that scales are **responding to anatomy**:
- scale orientation may reinforce ridges;
- scale size/shape may change across plane boundaries;
- articulation fields remain finer/flexible;
- structural fields may visually emphasize underlying form.

But scales must not create fake anatomy that disappears when texture is removed.

Bare geometry remains the primary test.

## 15. Cranial display structures — preserve TS5 success

The Cranial Keratin Display System is approved as a direction and should not be discarded.

TS5 successfully demonstrated useful variation:
- minimal/subtle;
- low hornlets;
- swept-back paired;
- crest-dominant;
- mixed/asymmetric;
- other configurations.

Continue using these only **after** the skull works.

Important: horn bases should respond to the new structural skull landscape. Attachment sites should look more convincing once orbital/temporal/posterior skull planes are improved.

Do not compensate for weak anatomy by increasing horn size.

## 16. Variation without species fragmentation

The structural architecture is a racial foundation, not one immutable head.

Future creator controls may vary:
- ridge prominence;
- plane transition strength;
- rostral breadth/depth;
- orbital rim strength;
- temporal definition;
- mandibular angle/depth;
- posterior skull contour.

However, randomization must preserve the Saurin identity envelope.

Do not create presets that look like separate unrelated reptile species unless a future population design explicitly calls for it.

## 17. Expression and dialogue safeguard

Structural angularity cannot encode permanent temperament.

Reject anatomy that forces:
- scowl;
- aggression;
- sinister expression;
- smile;
- sadness.

Test the bare neutral head mentally against future dialogue requirements.

The orbital and mouth systems must retain enough soft-tissue/articulation capacity for expressive animation.

## 18. Avoid the opposite failure

Do **not** overcorrect into:
- spikes everywhere;
- razor edges;
- excessive horns;
- fantasy-dragon armor plating;
- crocodile clone;
- dinosaur monster;
- faceted stone head;
- permanent combat silhouette.

The intended design language is:

**broad organic surfaces + decisive anatomical transitions.**

Not:
**every surface is sharp.**

## 20. TS6 acceptance tests

Reject TS6 if:
- bare geometry still looks like smooth human/mammal volumes with a snout;
- removing scales makes the reptilian identity collapse;
- removing horns makes the identity collapse;
- front view returns to eyes + nose + mouth mammalian organization;
- rostrum remains a single rounded wedge;
- orbit lacks structural integration;
- posterior skull remains an inflated sphere;
- mandible disappears into throat;
- sharpness comes only from added spikes;
- anatomy forces an angry expression;
- body remains uniformly cylindrical/rounded while only head changes;
- body becomes excessively armored/spiky;
- tail keratin is confused with baseline structural ridges;
- display morphology becomes a gameplay trait;
- any tail variant creates a larger damage target by design.
