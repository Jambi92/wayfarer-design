# Claude Directive — Saurin Targeted Sculpt 4: Jaw Architecture Pass

## 1. Review disposition

Targeted Sculpt 3 is an improvement over Targeted Sculpt 2, but the Saurin head is **not yet accepted**.

The orbit/eye treatment is moving in the correct direction: the eyes are better seated within the skull and the profile is less mascot-like. The dedicated non-human skull remains the correct foundation and must be preserved.

The principal failure is now the **mouth/jaw system**.

The current mouth reads as a groove or line cut around a single solid muzzle mass. In frontal view especially, the curved seam creates an unintended smile-like read. The large rounded central rostral/nasal pad also attracts attention as though it were a mammalian nose. The image does not yet communicate a convincing upper jaw, lower jaw, oral opening, posterior mandibular structure, and jaw hinge.

### Governing correction

> **Do not sculpt a mouth line. Sculpt an upper jaw and a lower jaw, then let their anatomical boundary create the mouth.**

This is the central requirement for Targeted Sculpt 4.

## 2. Build two articulated jaw masses

Treat the feeding apparatus as real anatomy.

### Upper jaw / rostral-maxillary complex

Construct the upper rostrum/maxillary region as its own structural volume:
- broad integrated root beneath/forward of the orbital platform;
- coherent dorsal, lateral and ventrolateral planes;
- moderate forward projection consistent with the approved rostral floor;
- structural taper toward the anterior end;
- sufficient depth for believable oral/dental anatomy without becoming a crocodilian snout;
- no human maxilla/nose/lip hierarchy.

The upper jaw must look capable of housing functional oral anatomy, not like a solid muzzle with a line engraved into it.

### Lower jaw / mandible

Construct a distinct lower-jaw volume:
- recognizable mandibular body beneath the oral boundary;
- increasing structural depth toward the posterior jaw;
- substantial posterior ramus/hinge region;
- credible relationship to the temporal/postorbital skull;
- natural transition into throat/neck;
- no human chin.

The lower jaw should visually appear capable of articulation even in a closed neutral pose.

### Oral opening

Only after the two jaw masses are established should the mouth boundary be defined.

The closed oral margin should:
- emerge naturally where upper and lower jaw tissues meet;
- avoid a ruler-straight cut;
- avoid the current broad smile-shaped curve;
- have subtle anatomical curvature and thickness variation;
- extend posteriorly toward the functional jaw hinge;
- retain a neutral resting expression;
- support eventual dialogue deformation.

Do not add human lips. Do not use an exaggerated exposed tooth row to disguise weak jaw anatomy.

## 3. Eliminate the mammalian-nose read

The current pale/rounded central rostral pad reads too strongly as a separate nose-like object.

Rebuild/integrate this region so:
- the anterior rostrum is one coherent reptilian structure;
- nostrils are openings within that structure;
- there is no bulbous mammalian nasal pad;
- no human nasal bridge, alae, philtrum or nose-over-mouth hierarchy returns;
- nostril placement and surrounding tissue look biologically integrated with the rostrum.

The anterior snout may have localized scale/keratin specialization, but it must not visually become a dog's nose, primate nose, beak, or decorative cap.

## 4. Strengthen posterior jaw and temporal mechanics

The projecting rostrum currently carries more visual authority than the rear feeding apparatus.

Strengthen:
- posterior mandibular depth;
- jaw hinge;
- temporal attachment region;
- transition from orbital/postorbital platform into jaw mechanics;
- soft-tissue/muscular volume appropriate to operating the jaw.

This should make the skull read as a complete functional system rather than "snout plus head."

Avoid exaggerated monster jaw muscles or a permanent predatory expression.

## 5. Mouth-edge tissue

Saurin do not need mammalian lips, but the oral margin should not look like a mathematically perfect incision.

Introduce restrained biological complexity:
- fine keratinized/scaled margin where appropriate;
- subtle changes in edge thickness;
- small local curvature changes;
- believable mouth-corner transition;
- integration into posterior jaw and throat.

Keep this subtle. The objective is believable tissue, not decorative ridges.

## 6. Preserve and continue TS3 orbital gains

Do not undo the improved eye seating.

Continue refining:
- supraorbital support;
- postorbital support;
- orbital-temporal integration;
- transition from orbital platform into rostral root.

Keep:
- forward-readable gaze;
- vertical pupils;
- adult proportions;
- neutral expression.

Avoid huge eyes, gecko/cute exaggeration, permanent scowl, or human brow anatomy.

## 7. Cranial and neck integration

Continue reducing any remaining blockout quality:
- refine low-to-moderate cranial vault into organic reptilian structure rather than a flat cap;
- preserve front-to-back cranial length;
- improve occipital transition;
- integrate jaw musculature, throat, cervical column and posterior skull;
- avoid a cylindrical neck carrying a separate head.

The complete head/neck should look biomechanically coherent with the projecting rostrum.

## 10. Target visual quality

The standing target remains:

> **The Saurin should look believable beside a photorealistic human—not like a human wearing reptilian prosthetics, and not like a cartoon lizard enlarged into human proportions.**

For the mouth specifically:

> **At neutral closure, an observer should be able to infer where the upper jaw ends, where the mandible begins, where the hinge operates, and how the mouth could open—without needing a dark line painted or carved around the muzzle to explain it.**

That is the Sculpt 4 success criterion.
