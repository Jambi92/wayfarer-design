# Part 1 Shared skeletal foundation and population divergence

## 2–3. Elven skeletal family and gracility (A)

Fenn, Aelari and Vael belong to a shared elven skeletal family that's genuinely distinct from the human populations. An elf is not a human skeleton with altered height, less mass and pointed ears, and the family must be recognizable through multiple relationships at once. This doesn't require one technical skeleton, since biology and implementation are separate questions. Elves are more gracile than equivalent humans, expressed through long-bone proportions, joint scale, wrists and ankles, shoulder articulation, hands and feet and overall skeletal mass. **Gracile doesn't mean fragile:** no lower strength, durability, health or athleticism and no mandatory thinness is implied, and gameplay effects stay unresolved.

## Waist-transition clarification (added to Part 1)

The phrase "longer waist transition" in the individual race specs referred to different proportional contexts, and is now normalized comparatively. The Fenn torso entry in the matrix above is read through this clarification.

| Population | Relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Fenn | Compact-centered torso, where the ribcage-to-pelvis transition may be relatively gradual or extended within the Fenn proportional system. This does not mean Fenn have a longer absolute or proportional waist transition than Aelari |
| Aelari | The greatest average vertical torso continuity of the three, including a longer average ribcage-to-pelvis (waist-transition) region than Fenn and Vael, which supports whole-body vertical elongation |
| Vael | Less vertical waist elongation than Aelari and stronger compact continuity between torso and pelvis. The deeper ribcage must not be mistaken for a longer waist |

The comparative shorthand for review and prototyping is: Fenn, a compact torso center with a relatively gradual transition within that compact system; Aelari, the greatest average vertical torso and waist continuity; Vael, a deeper torso with more compact torso-to-pelvis continuity. These are population tendencies with valid individual overlap.

**Terminology rule:** when comparing races, words such as "longer," "shorter," "deeper" or "broader" name their comparison target wherever ambiguity is possible.

## 23–24. Composition and frame (universal character rule)

No elven population is defined by low fat or low muscle. All three support low and high muscle, low and high fat, regional development, Narrow, Balanced and Broad frames, and age-related composition. **Broad frames are valid for all elves.** Broad means broader skeletal relationships within that population's range, never a human skeleton, Skarn or Durrim anatomy, automatic muscularity or automatic obesity.

## 25–26. Provisional shared-elven skeletal signal

The combination is greater gracility than humans, non-human torso, shoulder and clavicle relationships, a non-human pelvis foundation, elongated limbs, elven joints, elongated hands and fingers, and distinct feet. No single item defines an elf, since identity comes from the combined system. As shorthand for distributions, not rigid body types, Fenn are extremity-emphasized, Aelari are vertically distributed and Vael are deeper with compact continuity.

## 27–29. Validation and the anti-exaggeration rule

In the shared-elven neutralization test, matched Fenn, Aelari and Vael (ears, face for body-only tests, pigment, hair, clothing and presentation neutralized) are compared against matched humans. All three must read as one related non-human skeletal family and stay distinguishable as three populations without exaggeration, and both must succeed. In the equal-height test at identical height, frame, muscle, fat, age and pose, height isn't needed to classify them: Fenn show extremity contribution, Aelari vertical continuity and Vael deeper compact continuity. Differences aren't exaggerated for instant silhouette readability. The goal is believable shared ancestry, meaningful distributions and individual overlap, and perfect identification from every silhouette isn't required.

## 30. Technical architecture (E)

This review settles biology, not whether the elves use one skeleton, three skeletons, a shared hierarchy, different proportions, MetaHuman-derived architecture, custom meshes, retargeting, procedural deformation or hybrids. The architecture must later prove it can reproduce the approved anatomy, and implementation convenience never redefines the biology.

# Part 2 Craniofacial anatomy, eyes and external-ear ancestry

## 1–3. Craniofacial family and cranium (A)

Fenn, Aelari and Vael share a deeper non-human craniofacial ancestry. An elven face is not a human face with pointed ears, a reduced jaw and conventional attractiveness. Ancestry comes from combined relationships across the cranium, forehead, brow and orbits, midface, cheeks, nose, jaw and chin, eyes and ears, and no single feature defines an elf. **There is no universal elf face:** no mandatory eye, nose, jaw, chin, cheekbone, lip or brow shape, face width or attractiveness standard. A single "Elf Head" with superficial morphs for all three isn't used unless prototyping proves it can reproduce the approved diversity. The provisional shared cranial tendencies are somewhat greater cranial-height contribution than equivalent humans, different cranial-to-face relationships, elven integration of forehead, orbit and midface, and lower apparent craniofacial skeletal mass than robust human populations. Elves aren't defined by enlarged skulls.

The distributions overlap substantially. Aelari traits never equal beauty, refinement or nobility, Vael traits never equal harsh or villainous faces, permanent angularity or scowls, and Vael orbital structure must never create a permanent angry expression.

## 16–19. Eyes

These stay separate: orbital anatomy (skull and socket), external eye anatomy (lids, opening, spacing, angle, surrounding tissue), ocular anatomy (globe, iris, pupil, lens, retina), pigmentation (iris and sclera) and magical or supernatural effects (glow, magical color change).

| Topic | Classification | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared ocular ancestry | A + E | Shared deeper ocular ancestry, but no universal eye size, pupil diameter or shape, iris or scleral pigmentation, or low-light ability. Exact shared physiology waits for the Vael low-light work and prototyping |
| Pupil shape | E | Round pupils are the conservative baseline. There are no slit, vertical or horizontal pupils for exoticism, and any non-round pupil needs biological justification and cross-elf review |
| Vael low-light adaptation | D + E | A Vael-specific candidate specialization with the mechanism unresolved. It isn't retroactively given to Fenn or Aelari, and doesn't require larger eyes, glow, eye shine, slit pupils or daylight blindness. Gameplay stays OPEN |

## 20–29. External ears

The three share external-ear anatomy from a common non-human ancestral structure (A). Elven ears are not human ears stretched to a point, and the foundation covers skull attachment, ear base, cartilage, upper-ear form, taper, tip, projection, curvature and lobe. They may share a conceptual control family (length, base width, tip length, tip sharpness, vertical angle, sweep, lateral projection, curvature, lobe size and attachment) without identical ranges or morphs. The exact ancestral ear shape is E: no single ancestral elf ear is reconstructed yet, only the requirement that all three distributions be plausible descendants of a related foundation.

| Population | Ear tendency | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fenn (B) | Greater outward and backward projection, broad variation in orientation and length | No mandatory horizontal spear ears |
| Aelari (C) | More upward and backward orientation, somewhat closer-to-skull profile, moderate-to-long length, more gradual taper | Long elegant ears aren't mandatory |
| Vael (D) | Somewhat broader base, more lateral and backward orientation, moderately shorter taper than Aelari, strong skull-attachment integration | Never universally short, thick or bat-like |

**Ear overlap is locked.** One ear shouldn't reliably identify ancestry on its own: some Fenn have upward ears, some Aelari short ears, some Vael long ears. All three must stay recognizable with minimum ears, masked ears or hair-covered ears, or the face and body design is insufficient. Natural asymmetry stays separate from acquired damage (notches, tears, scars, missing tips, piercing damage), which never redefines ancestry, as a shared character-system principle. Ear mobility is E: tissue mechanics, musculature, animation value, emotional readability, headgear interaction and whether it's shared or population-specific are all still to be reviewed.

## 30–32. Aging and individuality

All three visibly age (a shared principle, A), in facial volume, skin elasticity, eye area, cheeks, jawline, neck, wrinkles, hair density and pigmentation and ear tissue. There's no permanent youth until extreme old age unless later lore establishes a mechanism. Lifespans, maturation, adult aging rates and maximum ages aren't assumed identical (E), pending a dedicated longevity review tied to lore. **Individual identity is locked:** every elven population supports plain, conventionally and unconventionally attractive, soft, rugged, narrow, broad, strong-jawed, strong-nosed, rounder, scarred, asymmetrical, high-body-fat and elderly individuals. No elven race is biologically required to be beautiful.

## 37–39. Part 2 conclusions

The provisional shared elven facial signal is a system, not one face shape: related non-human cranial relationships, orbital foundation and midface ancestry, gracile craniofacial construction relative to robust humans, related but diverse mandibles, and shared non-human ear ancestry, which each population then modifies. None of these is universally elven: tall stature, large or narrow eyes, small or narrow noses, high or hollow cheeks, pointed chins, narrow faces, long necks, pale skin, light or straight hair, blue or glowing eyes, long ears, conventional beauty, or graceful expression. These conclusions will inform Halvren later, but not as "move human sliders halfway toward elf." Mixed ancestry needs its own inheritance framework, which hasn't started.

## Part 2 comparative-terminology clarification

This names the comparison targets in Part 2 without changing any classification.

| Item | Clarified meaning | Does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fenn orbital and eye presentation (§4, §8, §33) | Fenn may trend toward a more open visible orbital and eye presentation on average than Aelari and Vael populations, with substantial overlap. "Open presentation" is the combined visible relationship of orbital anatomy, eyelids and exposed eye area, not eyeball size | Every Fenn has larger eyes or a larger visible opening, or any Fenn individual must have more open eyes than an Aelari or Vael individual |
| Mandibular mass (§13–14) | Shared: all three trend toward lower average apparent mandibular skeletal mass than equivalent robust human populations. | Fenn ear projection (§23) | Fenn may trend toward greater average lateral (outward) projection from the skull than Aelari and Vael populations, often combined with backward sweep. **Comparative anatomy terminology rule (strengthened, applies to all race specs and comparative reviews going forward):** comparative anatomical language names either the comparison population or the anatomical reference relationship being measured. For example, "greater average thoracic depth than Fenn and Aelari" rather than "deeper torso"; "greater average lateral ear projection from the skull than Aelari and Vael" rather than "more projected ears"; and "longer forearm relative to total arm length" rather than "longer forearms" when the comparison is proportional. Measurements that are easily conflated stay distinct: absolute versus proportional length, width versus depth, projection versus sweep, skeletal versus muscular mass, orbital size versus visible eye opening, and body size versus body composition.

# Part 3 Pigmentation, hair, population variation and environmental adaptation

## 1–4. Pigmentation and skin foundation

Shared ancestry doesn't require shared modern pigmentation (A, shared biological principle). Fenn, Aelari and Vael needn't share skin, undertone, hair or iris pigmentation, since divergence can reshape distributions over long periods, and **none of the three is the pigmentation baseline.** The ancestral elven palette is E: it isn't reconstructed unless world history or mixed-ancestry design needs it, and modern populations may have preserved, lost, shifted or independently developed their distributions. All three have living biological skin (pigmentation, undertone, perfusion where appropriate, local anatomical, age and environmental variation, acquired marks), but there's no universal "elven skin material" forcing identical visible properties. Physiological and material differences are E, pending prototyping. The Natural, Environmental and Applied/Acquired skin layers are shared character-system concepts, not identical skin biology.

**Pigmentation isn't identity (locked):** Vael, Fenn and Aelari identity must all survive pigmentation neutralization at the population level. Pigmentation contributes to appearance, and never replaces anatomy.

## 9–10. Lighting

Lighting invariance is a universal character principle (A): stored biological appearance and observed lighting are separate, so lighting changes the render, never the pigmentation. It applies to Fenn, Aelari, Vael and all future playable races where applicable. Visual development sets neutral reference conditions for skin, hair, eyes and natural markings first, then tests the same appearance in warm, cool, daylight, overcast, firelight, moonlight, low and magical or environmental light. Biological colors are never designed by sampling stylized scene lighting.

## 16–18. Iris color and magical eye effects

Biological iris appearance stays separate from magical or supernatural eye effects, and no elf is biologically required to have glowing eyes. There's no universal elven iris palette: brown, dark brown, amber, hazel-like, green, gray, blue, blue-gray, green-gray, muted violet and other grounded variants are possible across populations where validated, with per-population validity and frequency subject to visual review. Magical effects (glow or emission, temporary color or pattern change, supernatural opacity, other magical states) aren't biological pigmentation. They belong to magic and gameplay systems unless lore establishes a biological condition, and never define ancestry.

## 19–23. Environmental adaptation

Four concepts are locked as separate: **ancestral biology** (inherited from deeper ancestry), **population-level environmental adaptation** (heritable change from long-term environmental pressure), **individual environmental exposure** (changes from one person's lived environment) and **culture** (learned behavior, technology, traditions, social systems).

| Population | Classification | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fenn | B + E | A long-term population association with forests, but existing biological and gameplay traits aren't assumed to come from every forest lifestyle. Whether a trait is deep elven ancestry, Fenn-inherited adaptation, learned skill or gameplay abstraction stays unresolved. The existing sneak and swim gameplay traits are kept for later balance review and not resolved here |
| Aelari | C + E | History may have shaped distributions, but tower-dwelling, scholarship, arcane study, formality, "wisdom" and social status aren't biological. No major Aelari environmental adaptation is locked |
| Vael | D + E | Enhanced low-light adaptation stays a serious Vael-specific candidate, mechanism unresolved (pupil response, retinal or photoreceptor sensitivity, light gathering, neural processing, fantasy equivalents). Eye shine, tapetum-like structures, slit pupils, giant eyes, daylight weakness, color-vision tradeoffs and gameplay bonuses aren't finalized |

Adaptation isn't culture. A surface-raised Vael may keep inherited Vael ocular biology, a city-raised Fenn keeps Fenn anatomy, and an Aelari raised among Marchfolk keeps Aelari anatomy, since culture and birthplace don't rewrite ancestry.

## 24–27. Population variation

Validity isn't frequency (A, universal population-generation principle): a valid trait can be Very Common, Common, Uncommon or Rare, and rarity never blocks manual player selection. **Overlap is locked:** related elven populations may share individual traits (similar Fenn and Aelari pigmentation, similar Aelari and Vael hair color, shared iris colors, overlapping hair textures and ear shapes), and no single cosmetic trait perfectly classifies race. Soft probabilistic correlations are allowed where justified, never rigid packages such as pale skin, blond hair and blue eyes; gray skin, white hair and violet eyes; or warm skin, brown hair and green eyes. Regional or geographic subpopulations with somewhat different frequencies (region, climate, migration, isolation, contact) are a future principle (A), not designed yet, and a subpopulation doesn't automatically equal a culture.

## 33. Halvren implications

Halvren pigmentation isn't human plus elven divided by two, and hair texture, hair color, iris color and undertone aren't averaged either. Inheritance may involve overlapping distributions, dominance, polygenic expression and setting-specific biology, and needs its own mixed-ancestry framework, not designed yet.

## 34–35. Part 3 conclusions

**There is currently no universal modern elven complexion, hair color or eye color (locked).** The strongest shared-elven identity is anatomical, not cosmetic: pigmentation, hair and eye distributions may support population identity but never replace anatomy. The open items are exact Fenn, Aelari and Vael pigmentation frequencies, undertone, hair texture, hair color and iris frequencies, ancestral elven pigmentation, detailed skin physiology, the Vael low-light mechanism, Vael bright-light response and geographic subpopulations.

# Part 4 Aging, movement, technical architecture and final shared-ancestry matrix

## 1–3. Aging and lifecycle

All three visibly age (A, shared principle), and ancestry doesn't require permanent youth. Age can affect skin elasticity, facial volume, eye region, cheeks, jawline, neck, wrinkling, hair density and pigmentation, body composition, posture where individually appropriate, and ear tissue where appropriate. Old elves are never just young elves with white hair. Lifespan, maximum and average age, maturation, adult aging rate, fertility span and whether all three age at the same rate are E: shared ancestry makes related lifecycles plausible without proving identical modern lifespans, and they need a dedicated lifecycle and lore review. Biological age (physical aging) stays separate from age presentation (hair styling, clothing, grooming, cosmetics, cultural presentation), and culture doesn't determine biological age appearance.

## 4–5. Shared biomechanics and movement layers

Related non-human anatomy may give related biomechanics (A), arising from limb proportions, joints, center of mass, torso, shoulders, pelvis, hands and feet and skeletal mass distribution, but there's no universal "elf animation style." Five layers are locked as distinct: **anatomical biomechanics** (from body structure), **learned movement** (training, occupation, experience), **cultural body language** (socially learned posture and gesture), **personal body language** (personality and habit) and **emotional state** (temporary). Personality and culture are never encoded into racial locomotion.

## 6–8. Movement by population

## 9–10. Movement testing and gameplay

The equal-body-condition test compares matched individuals across the three populations for height where ranges overlap, frame, muscle, fat, age, training, emotional state and movement task, isolating anatomy from composition and performance style. A biomechanical difference doesn't automatically justify faster movement, higher jumps, better stealth, balance or dodging, higher stamina or attack speed. Visual movement and mechanical performance stay separate until explicitly linked by gameplay review.

## 11–14. Technical architecture (E)

Biological relationship doesn't dictate one skeleton, three skeletons, a shared hierarchy, MetaHuman, modified MetaHuman, custom meshes, one or separate morph systems, procedural deformation, retargeting or any hybrid. Architecture must reproduce the approved anatomy, not redefine it. The populations still share a unified conceptual character system where practical (appearance data, age, frame, composition, pigmentation, hair, facial and ear controls, randomization, presets, save and load, schema and version tracking, migration) without identical meshes or skeletons. **MetaHuman is a candidate only** for all three elves and must be validated against approved anatomy, and any approach that needs substantial approved biological differences removed to fit the tool is rejected. Technical prototyping will test shared, modified shared, shared-hierarchy-with-proportions, population-specific and hybrid skeletons against shoulders, ribcage, spine, pelvis, limb proportions, hands, feet, neck, cranium, ears, deformation, IK, retargeting and equipment fitting. No architecture is chosen in this review.

## 15–17. Equipment, world and combat

Equipment accommodates approved anatomy without scaling every item, since canonical equipment dimensions don't scale with holder size. Armor, gloves, boots, headgear, ear accommodation, hair-headgear interaction, weapon grips, hand placement, deformation and genuinely required race variants are reviewed later. All three elves stay subject to playable-race world compatibility (doors, ceilings, stairs, chairs, beds, ladders, tables, counters, tunnels, interaction points, cameras, mounts). Aelari maximum height needs particular attention, but the approved range isn't changed for prototype convenience. Whether major racial differences affect collision, melee, interaction or combat reach, movement speed, camera position or hit detection is E, an open gameplay and technical decision. Individual cosmetic variation within a race grants no automatic advantage or penalty, and race-level differences need explicit balance review.

## 32–34. Final validation standard

**Related but distinct.** Representative populations should look biologically related, never identical except for color or ears, three unrelated fantasy species, or human presets with pointed ears. The goal is three recognizable populations descended from a shared non-human biological family. **Overlap isn't failure (locked).** An unusual Fenn may resemble some Aelari, an unusual Aelari some Vael, and an unusual Vael may share several Fenn traits. Identity emerges statistically across many anatomical relationships, and perfect classification of isolated individuals isn't required. **Exaggeration isn't the fix.** If readability is weak in prototypes, check first that the approved multi-region anatomy is actually implemented correctly, before enlarging ears or eyes or pushing skin color, angularity, height or jaw shape.

# Final consistency clarification

This is part of the canonical review set, and doesn't otherwise alter the completed review. The final matrix above has been updated to match it.

## 1. Skeletal gracility ordering

| Population | Relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Fenn | Greatest average skeletal gracility of the three elven populations |
| Aelari | Strong average skeletal gracility, with somewhat greater average skeletal structural presence than Fenn when comparing otherwise equivalent individuals |
| Vael | Generally gracile relative to equivalent robust human populations, with the greatest average joint, base and skeletal structural presence of the three |

The provisional average ordering, from greater gracility toward greater structural presence, is **Fenn, then Aelari, then Vael.** It's a population distribution with substantial overlap, not a rigid rule: an individual Aelari may be more gracile than an individual Fenn, and an individual Vael more gracile than an individual Aelari. It never becomes three fixed bone-thickness presets. Skeletal gracility never means weakness, low muscularity, low body weight, frailty, a small frame or low physical capability, and it's one anatomical variable within the larger body system.

## 2. Aelari neck length

Aelari trend toward greater average neck length, and greater neck contribution to total vertical body proportion, than Fenn and Vael populations, with substantial overlap. Not every Aelari has a longer neck than every Fenn or Vael. **Absolute neck length** (measured length) and **proportional neck contribution** (neck length relative to the individual's total body proportions) both may shape the Aelari vertical silhouette, but they aren't interchangeable measurements.

## 3. Ear projection from the skull

Aelari ears trend toward lower average lateral projection away from the skull than Fenn ears. Against Vael the distributions may overlap substantially, with no strict universal ranking yet.

| Population | Projection relationship |
| --- | --- |
| Fenn | Greatest average lateral (outward) projection of the three |
| Aelari | Relatively low lateral projection, with greater upward and backward orientation |
| Vael | An overlapping, intermediate or broad distribution, characterized more by a broader average ear base and lateral and backward orientation than by one fixed projection value |

"Closer to skull" isn't a separate ear shape. Projection, sweep, vertical angle, base width, taper and length stay separate anatomical variables.

## 4–5. Aelari approved height compared with the current prototype

**KNOWN CURRENT-IMPLEMENTATION / TARGET-DESIGN GAP.** The prototype Aelari use a 1.02 race scale with about ±7.5% generic height variation, which doesn't reproduce the approved range of 168–221 cm (reference about 190 cm). The approved range stays authoritative: the maximum isn't reduced to fit the prototype, and whole-character scale and the height system aren't changed yet. The current system stays a placeholder under Approved Design Specification > Open Decision Register > Prototype Implementation.

When character and world prototyping is authorized, approved maximum-height Aelari (about 221 cm, 7'3") join permanent validation against doors, doorframes, ceilings and low ceilings, stairs, ladders, chairs, beds, tables, counters, tunnels, interaction points, conversation framing, the third-person camera, first-person if applicable, equipment, weapons, mounts, collision, navigation, animation and IK. The approved minimum and reference heights are tested too, and world compatibility is never validated only against sizes the prototype can currently reach.

## 6. Cross-race world validation principle (strengthened, all playable races)

**World compatibility is validated against approved target anatomy, not just anatomy currently reachable in the prototype character creator.** A prototype limitation must never silently become a world-design constraint, and every approved anatomical extreme is tested before final world metrics are locked.
