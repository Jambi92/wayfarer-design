# 6. FAMILY A — REQUIRED S3 FORM

Keep the concept of:

- a broad sternal/anterior structural plane;
- distinct lateral thoracic planes;
- a readable costal structural border;
- a deep Gorrund trunk.

But rebuild the geometry so those are **anatomical planes on a radiused section**, not a box.

## Required changes from S2

### A1. Radius the anterior ↔ lateral transitions

Remove the long hard vertical box corners.

The transition should read as:

- broad anterior plane;
- one or more intermediate oblique/radiused transition zones;
- lateral thoracic plane.

The transition may be strong.

It may not look machined.

### A2. Remove the lower-trunk "belt" ledge

The costal/lower structural border must not form a continuous shelf that visually cuts the torso from the pelvis.

Preserve a readable lower structural border, but continue the trunk volume through the lower trunk into the pelvis.

The body must not look like:

- a chest block placed on a pelvic block;
- a cuirass;
- a belt shelf;
- stacked slabs.

### A3. Rework the S2 chest-plate top border

The S2 V-shaped / hard top transition crowds the pectoral area and produces an unhealthy fold in low composition.

Replace it with a broader anatomical transition carried through:

- sternum;
- clavicular/upper-costal region;
- shoulder-girdle integration.

Do not create a human pectoral shelf.

Do not create a sharp V armor plate.

### A4. Preserve depth without belly/bulk

The trunk must stay deep because of structural rib-cage organization.

It must not gain its read from:

- belly projection;
- soft chest mass;
- obesity;
- muscle hypertrophy.

At low composition, the same hard-form organization must remain.

### A5. Suppress human surface cues

Within permitted trunk anatomy, subordinate:

- nipple relief;
- navel relief;
- conventional pectoral shelf;
- human waist cue.

Do not erase them into an anatomical void; simply prevent them from controlling the read.
