**Scope:** Saurin head anatomy and cranial keratin display structures. Preserve successful TS4 gains. No UE5 implementation.

## 1. Tyler's direction

Tyler has requested two linked changes:

1. **Use actual reptile heads as anatomical reference** to improve the Saurin skull, rostrum, jaws, mouth, orbit, and head/neck integration.
2. Introduce a broader **cranial keratin display system**—horns, hornlets, ridges, crests, spikes, and plate-like growths—which can perform the individual-customization role that hairstyles perform for races with scalp hair.

This supersedes the earlier overly restrictive Saurin assumption that true horns are not baseline-capable and that cranial display is limited to low-profile ridges.

The change does **not** mean every Saurin must have large horns.

## 2. Reference real reptiles — synthesize, do not copy

Before TS5 sculpting, deliberately examine real reptile cranial anatomy from multiple useful reference families.

Useful reference categories include:
- monitor lizards / varanids — jaw mechanics, elongated rostrum, skull-to-neck relationship;
- iguanids — orbital structure, cranial display anatomy, scale organization;
- bearded dragons / agamids — skull planes, orbital integration, keratinous display structures;
- crocodilians — separation of upper jaw and mandible, posterior jaw mechanics, long-rostrum structural logic;
- horned lizards and other reptiles with cranial projections — how display structures emerge from believable cranial regions.

Do **not** replicate a single living species.

The Saurin remain their own fantasy humanoid lineage. Reference study is for biomechanical and morphological logic.

Avoid:
- upright monitor-lizard copy;
- crocodile head pasted onto a humanoid;
- iguana cosplay;
- dragon default;
- dinosaur caricature;
- mammalian muzzle;
- human face under reptile texture.

## 3. TS5 skull objective

TS4 is a meaningful improvement and should **not** be discarded.

Preserve:
- improved eye/orbit seating;
- stronger reptilian profile;
- improved upper/lower jaw separation;
- approved rostral projection direction;
- forward-readable gaze;
- vertical pupils;
- adult proportions.

TS5 should refine the existing foundation using real reptile anatomical logic.

### Primary remaining failures

#### A. Remove the mammalian nasal-button read

The rounded anterior central pad remains too visually separate and reads like a dog/bear/primate nose.

Replace it with a coherent anterior rostral termination:
- nostrils integrated directly into rostral anatomy;
- broader/flatter or otherwise structurally continuous reptilian terminal geometry;
- no bulbous separate nose object;
- no philtrum;
- no human nasal bridge/ala hierarchy;
- no mammalian nose-over-mouth facial organization.

#### B. Strengthen the mandible

The lower jaw still loses too much structural authority into the throat.

Refine:
- mandibular body;
- posterior mandibular depth;
- ramus/hinge;
- temporal attachment;
- jaw musculature;
- jaw-to-throat transition.

It should be possible to understand how the jaw opens from bare geometry.

#### C. Correct the frontal mouth read

The front mouth remains too horizontal/mechanical.

Do not sculpt a smile or frown to solve it.

Instead use actual jaw geometry so the oral boundary emerges naturally from:
- upper-jaw volume;
- mandibular volume;
- rostral taper;
- mouth-corner transition;
- posterior hinge placement.

#### D. Preserve three-dimensional rostrum structure in front view

Profile is now substantially stronger than front view. The rostrum must not collapse into a button when seen head-on.

Cranial display structures are an enhancement, not a disguise for unresolved skull anatomy.

## 5. New canonical biology — Cranial Keratin Display System

Saurin now possess a species-specific **Cranial Keratin Display System**.

Canonical principle:

> **Saurin possess a species-specific cranial keratin display system. Expression ranges from subtle ridges and low hornlets through prominent horns, crests, spikes and plate-like growths. Configuration varies substantially between individuals and serves as the Saurin character-creation analogue to hairstyle without encoding sex, culture, personality, class or combat capability. These structures grow from anatomically valid cranial attachment regions and must remain mechanically integrated with the underlying skull. Minimal expression is valid, but mammalian scalp hair is not part of baseline Saurin biology.**

This replaces the earlier limitation to only low-profile keratinous ridges.

### Important interpretation

The **capacity** for these structures is racial biology.

The exact expression is individual variation.

Valid adult expressions may include:
- nearly flat/minimal cranial ridges;
- small hornlets;
- paired posterior horns;
- swept-back horns;
- brow-region hornlets where anatomically valid;
- crown arrangements;
- posterior skull spikes;
- central or paired crests;
- plate-like keratin growths;
- mixed ridge/horn configurations;
- asymmetrical natural growth within valid bounds;
- acquired broken/worn/chipped structures.

Do not turn these into fixed subraces.

## 6. Hair-equivalent creator role

For Saurin, this system should occupy much of the **identity/customization role** normally served by hairstyle.

The creator may eventually expose controls/presets for:
- structure family;
- number/count where biologically valid;
- attachment region;
- spacing;
- base dimensions;
- length;
- thickness;
- taper;
- sweep;
- curvature;
- orientation;
- symmetry/asymmetry;
- crest height/profile;
- plate morphology;
- horn/ridge surface morphology;
- wear/damage as acquired appearance.

This is a design requirement, not a UE5 UI implementation instruction.

Do not label these structures as literal human hair.

Human Hair and Facial Hair categories remain biologically empty for baseline Saurin; race-aware creator navigation may substitute Cranial Display controls.

## 7. Biology vs presentation

Preserve the project's separation of biology and presentation.

### Biological / Natural
- horn/ridge/crest/plate growth;
- natural keratin morphology;
- natural coloration;
- inherited arrangement tendencies;
- age-related growth/wear where later defined.

### Acquired
- breakage;
- chips;
- scars at attachment areas;
- asymmetric damage;
- wear.

### Applied / Personal Presentation
Future Saurin may:
- polish structures;
- paint/dye them;
- wrap them;
- attach jewelry;
- add caps/bands;
- apply decorative treatments;
- possibly carve superficial mature keratin if later validated as safe/biologically plausible.

Applied decoration is **not inherited biology** and must not be randomized as Biological Anatomy.

## 8. Gameplay firewall

Cranial display structures are primarily biological identity/customization features.

Do **not** automatically infer:
- headbutt attacks;
- gore attacks;
- bonus armor;
- intimidation;
- magic;
- social rank;
- sex;
- caste;
- profession;
- personality;
- intelligence;
- aggression.

Large structures must not automatically punish the player through expanded damage vulnerability. Combat hitbox/collision treatment remains a later gameplay/technical decision consistent with the existing principle that distinctive Saurin anatomy should not become an arbitrary player punishment.

## 9. Sex and population firewall

Do not make one horn arrangement "male" and another "female" unless later biology explicitly establishes such dimorphism.

For first-pass Saurin:
- overlapping expression across sexes remains valid;
- sex does not silently determine horns, frame, rostrum, pigmentation, or personality;
- future population tendencies may weight display morphology but do not create fixed biological subraces.

## 10. Mechanical integration

Display structures must emerge from plausible cranial regions.

They cannot look glued onto a human skull.

For every prominent structure, consider:
- underlying cranial support;
- base footprint;
- growth direction;
- mass;
- neck/head balance;
- nearby orbit and jaw mechanics;
- skin/scale-to-keratin transition;
- collision with the character's own head/neck;
- plausible relationship to helmets/headgear later.

Do not let display structures destroy the approved orbital, rostral, jaw, or auricular architecture.
