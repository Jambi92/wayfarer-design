## Prompt

You are an independent visual reviewer for a fantasy game's body-reference roster. You have NO other context. Do not search any repository, file, or the web; look ONLY at this one image with the Read tool:

/home/claude/stc/c41/blind/front_only.jpg

It shows four neutral grey reference bodies, labelled W, X, Y, Z, seen ONLY from the front, all at about the same height. Some may be different humanoid species, some may be the same species. Do not guess names or intended species. Treat any flat shading patches as rendering, not anatomy, unless you think they show real shape.

Answer concisely:
1. Can the bodies be distinguished structurally from the front view alone? Which ones read as the same kind of creature/species and which as different?
2. For each body, what front-view cues drive your read (name specific anatomical features: shoulders, ribcage, waist, pelvis, limb lengths and thickness, joints, hands, neck, head)?
3. For every pair you consider different: does the difference rest mainly on (a) overall size, (b) thinness vs breadth, or (c) anatomical organization (proportions / structure)? Name the features.
4. Does any body read as a stretched human or a caricature? Which and why? ("none" if none.)
5. If you had to pick the single body that is LEAST human, which is it and how confident are you (low / medium / high)?

## Answer (verbatim)

I looked only at the one image. W, Y and Z read as the same kind of creature. X is the one that stands apart.

**1. Can they be told apart from the front alone?**
Partly.
- W, Y and Z read as one species shown in three builds, from slim to broad. Their joints, heads, necks, waists and limb lengths all sit at about the same heights.
- X is the only one that reads as built differently, but the front-view cues for it are moderate rather than striking.

**2. What drives the read for each body**
- **W:** Narrowest frame of the group. Shoulders are rounded and moderately square, and the ribcage tapers to a mild waist. The pelvis is narrow, the limbs are slim and tubular, and the neck is short. Normal human proportions: the hands end around mid-thigh and the crotch is at about half the height.
- **X:**
  - The shoulders slope down from a longer, thinner neck, so the head sits higher above the shoulders.
  - The torso is a narrow, nearly straight-sided block with little waist, sitting on a boxy pelvis and upper thigh.
  - The arms look longer and thinner, with knobby elbows. The forearms are long and the hands hang lower, toward the lower third of the thigh.
  - The legs are thin with prominent knees, and the shins are long and narrow.
  - The darker bands across the chest and the hip and thigh look like rendering, but the blocky edge at the hip does seem to show real shape.
- **Y:** W's layout with wider, squarer shoulders, a fuller chest, more limb thickness and slightly wider hips. The joint heights match W's.
- **Z:** The broadest body. It has the widest shoulders, a deep chest, a wider pelvis and heavy thighs, with thick upper arms and calves. Joint positions and head size are still the same as W and Y.

**3. What each difference rests on**
- **W vs Y and W vs Z:** Mainly (b), thinness versus breadth, plus some (a) overall bulk. The proportions don't change.
- **Y vs Z:** (b). Z is the same layout with more breadth in the thighs, hips and shoulders.
- **X vs W, Y or Z:** Mainly (c), how the body is put together. The features are the sloped shoulders, longer neck, longer arms with lower-hanging hands, straight-sided waistless torso, boxy pelvis, and long thin shins with knobby joints. There is some (b) as well, since X is also thin.

**4. Stretched human or caricature?**
X slightly leans toward a stretched human. Much of what separates it is elongation and thinning: longer neck, arms and shins. The sloped shoulders, boxy pelvis and joint knobs do add real structure beyond that. Z leans mildly toward a "bodybuilder" caricature of the same base as W and Y, but it reads as a heavier build, not a different creature. None of them is an outright caricature.

**5. Least human body**
X, with medium confidence. The front view shows its proportions and silhouette but not depth, posture or how the joints work, so a side view would be needed to confirm it.
