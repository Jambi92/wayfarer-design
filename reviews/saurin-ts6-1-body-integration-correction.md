## 2. Main torso/body remains too rounded and human-soft

Tyler's additional observation is important: even with scales, the TS6.1 body remains very rounded, smooth, and mammalian/human in its large forms. The Saurin head is developing sharper reptilian plane changes, but the torso and limbs do not yet speak the same anatomical language.

We need a controlled extension of the TS6 cranial design principle into the body.

This does NOT mean making the character thin, armored, spiky everywhere, emaciated, or dragon-like. It means the Saurin's major forms should have more structured planar transitions and less continuous human-style soft rounding.

## 3. Introduce structural planes and ridges into the body

Develop a restrained reptilian structural vocabulary using skeletal landmarks, musculotendinous boundaries, scale-field transitions, and low-profile integumentary ridges.

Candidate regions to explore:

The body should gain purposeful changes of direction. Broad surfaces can remain, but they should meet through clearer anatomical transitions rather than being blended into a uniformly rounded mannequin.

Think: broad plane -> ridge/transition -> adjacent plane, not sphere -> tube -> sphere.

## 4. Preserve biological plausibility and articulation

Do not turn every edge into a hard blade. Areas that must flex—axilla, elbow, wrist, lower trunk, hip, knee, ankle, tail articulation—still need compliant transition fields consistent with Regional Scale Architecture.

Structural ridges should be subordinate to movement. They cannot imply rigid external armor unless that is separately approved.

The correct target is a living reptilian humanoid whose anatomy has sharper organization, not a creature wearing an exoskeleton.
