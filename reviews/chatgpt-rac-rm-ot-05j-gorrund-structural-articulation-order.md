# 2. DESIGN DIAGNOSIS — THE IDENTITY LOAD IS IN THE WRONG PLACE

S2 and S3 have now demonstrated the failure mode clearly:

- too much planar trunk emphasis → **box / slab / rectangular** read;
- enough rounding to satisfy the trunk guardrails → **heavy / obese human** read.

Therefore:

> Stop trying to make the Gorrund identity come primarily from the outer trunk silhouette.

The canonical identity is broader than the trunk.

Gorrund are defined by:

- massive load-bearing skeletal architecture;
- high absolute structural scale;
- broad / deep axial construction;
- strong axial-to-limb load transfer;
- substantial joints;
- strong thorax / girdle / pelvis integration;
- structural massiveness that survives low muscle and low fat.

S4 must distribute the non-human read across the **whole load-bearing system**.

## A1. Sternal / anterior structural read

Create a clearer sternum / parasternal organization through:

- shallow plane hierarchy;
- subtle costosternal transitions;
- controlled changes in surface normal;
- superior/inferior continuity.

Do not create:

- armor plate;
- embossed sternum;
- hard V;
- square chest panel;
- human pectoral shelf.

## A2. Costal architecture

Make the inferior costal region more legible as a rib-cage structure without creating a shelf.

Desired:

- lower ribs handing off into the lower axial trunk;
- no horizontal ledge;
- no belly overhang.

The costal transition should be evident in front 3/4 and profile.

## A3. Rib-cage depth must read as skeleton

Keep substantial depth, but shape it so observers read:

- deep thorax;
- load-bearing rib cage;
- axial structure;

not:

- fat chest;
- soft barrel;
- obese torso.

Use surface organization, not added volume, to create this distinction.
