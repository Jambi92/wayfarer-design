The latest cleanup is a major improvement, but the Saurin is **not frozen yet**.

Two remaining anatomical reads are not accepted:

1. The orbital/postorbital region still contains a conspicuous raised mass around the eye socket. The postorbital peg was improved, but the region still reads too much like a bump applied to the skull instead of a continuous cranial plane/load path.
2. The tail still loses proximal mass too quickly. It is better than earlier versions, but it still reads as a thick root transitioning into a comparatively thin tail over too short a distance.

A third controlled exploration is now authorized:

3. Introduce a **larger structural reptilian scale/scute hierarchy** above the existing Regional Scale Architecture to determine whether stronger scale-size hierarchy improves Saurin identity and reinforces anatomical flow.

**Critical rule:** scales may reinforce corrected anatomy; they may **not hide unresolved anatomy**. Correct orbit and tail form first. Structural-scale work comes after those corrections.

## 3. Correction A — orbital/postorbital integration

### Problem

The current brow/postorbital/jugal solution is mechanically continuous on paper, but visually the rear/upper orbital structure still reads as a localized rounded protrusion or knob sitting on the side of the skull.

We need **plane transition, not a bump**.

### Required change

Rework only the authorized periorbital/postorbital region so the eye is embedded within a continuous cranial architecture.

Target flow:

**brow/supraorbital plane → lateral orbital rim → postorbital plane/ridge → temporal/jugal plane → quadrate/jaw-hinge region**

Requirements:

- Remove the isolated rounded/bulbous read immediately posterior/superior to the orbit.
- Broaden the transition into the surrounding skull rather than simply lowering the existing bump.
- Prefer a shallow planar shelf/ridge with directional sweep over a hemispherical boss.
- The postorbital structure may remain sharp where biologically useful, but its **base must dissolve into neighboring cranial planes**.
- Preserve the eye aperture and eye position.
- Preserve the accepted rostrum.
- Do not make the skull smoother or more human.
- Do not solve this by adding a horn, ridge ornament or scale cluster over the problem.
- In pure profile and front 3/4, there should be no obvious "button," "peg," or rounded socket mass.
- In top view, brow-to-temporal width should change progressively rather than through a local swelling.
- Maintain a believable structural route into the jugal/quadrate region.

### Diagnostic requirement

Show identical-camera:

- front;
- pure profile;
- front 3/4;
- rear 3/4;
- top;
- tight orbital close-up;
- before/after overlay or alternating comparison;
- curvature/normal diagnostic if useful.

Explicitly answer:

> Does the orbital region now read as the skull changing planes around the eye, rather than a feature attached to the skull?

If no, iterate before presenting the package.

## 4. Correction B — tail mass-decay curve

### Problem

The tail remains too abrupt in its reduction of cross-sectional mass. The proximal root is substantial, but the body reaches the thinner distal regime too quickly.

The desired read is one long counterbalancing organ that **continuously sheds mass from pelvis to tip**.

### Required change

Keep:

- overall tail length;
- tip position;
- general path/curvature;
- pelvic origin;
- accepted tail-root integration.

Change only the cross-sectional mass distribution along the free tail.

Requirements:

- Extend the substantial/intermediate mass farther distally.
- Reduce the peak rate of area loss in the early-to-middle tail.
- Redistribute that loss across a longer distance.
- Avoid simply making the entire tail uniformly thicker.
- Preserve a fine distal tip.
- Avoid a cylindrical middle.
- Avoid a second abrupt taper farther down the tail.
- Preserve dorsal/ventral and lateral anatomical flow from pelvis through root.
- The tail should read as a continuous muscular counterbalance, not "thick base + skinny appendage."

Quantify cross-sectional area along normalized tail length and provide a taper-rate graph/table. Compare directly with the current accepted cleanup.

Desired qualitative curve:

- proximal: strong mass, slow initial decay;
- early/mid: long progressive reduction;
- distal: continued smooth reduction;
- terminal: fine controlled tip.

Do not alter tail length to obtain the result.

## 5. Structural scale/scute hierarchy exploration

Only after A and B are anatomically satisfactory, explore a second scale hierarchy above the existing fine Regional Scale Architecture.

This is **not** permission to turn the Saurin into a generic dragon.

### Design principle

Use at least two visibly different orders of integument:

1. **fine/flexible scale fields** — articulation, expression and high-mobility regions;
2. **larger structural scales/scutes** — selected protective/load-bearing/dorsal regions.

The existing small-scale system remains useful. We are adding regional hierarchy, not replacing the entire body with large armor tiles.

### Candidate larger-scale regions

Prioritize controlled tests on:

- supraorbital/brow and temporal cranial planes;
- dorsal/posterolateral cranium;
- nape;
- upper dorsal thorax / shoulder transition;
- selected dorsal forearm;
- selected dorsal shin;
- dorsal and dorsolateral tail;
- lateral tail root where it helps communicate pelvic-to-tail flow.

Keep scales finer around:

- eyelids;
- mouth margin;
- jaw articulation;
- throat flexion;
- axilla;
- elbow and knee flexion;
- wrist/ankle articulation;
- palmar/plantar contact regions;
- other high-deformation areas.

### Tail-specific scale gradient

Test a biologically coherent scale-size gradient:

- larger/heavier structural scales near root/proximal dorsal tail;
- progressively smaller units moving distally;
- transition must be gradual;
- orientation should follow tail force/flow;
- no visible belt where scale family changes;
- no repeating procedural "dragon armor" rows.

This gradient should **reinforce**, not fake, the improved geometric taper.

### Cranial scales

Use structural scale orientation/size to emphasize the corrected brow → temporal → jugal flow.

Do not recreate the rejected orbital bump with oversized scales.

Fine expressive scales must remain around the eye aperture.
