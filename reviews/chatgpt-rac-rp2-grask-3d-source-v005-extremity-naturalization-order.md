## 4. Primary correction A — hand mass and shape language

The v004 hand is structurally successful but visually too narrow, bony, and mechanically segmented.

Current concern:
- fingers read as straight or slightly curved shafts with rounded joint bands;
- PIP/DIP transitions resemble collars or beads;
- digit shafts are too cylindrical;
- the hand reads more long/bony than broad/powerful;
- some web spaces are too clean or trench-like;
- the thumb still needs stronger integration into the thenar/palm mass.

### Required

Naturalize the hand while preserving its v004 overall scale.

#### 4.1 Finger shaft mass
Increase local finger-body mass modestly so the digits feel:
- thick;
- load-bearing;
- powerful;
- Grask-specific;
- capable of heavy weapon grip.

Do not simply scale all fingers uniformly.

Use anatomical taper:
- broader proximal phalanx;
- moderate middle phalanx;
- controlled distal taper;
- fingertip still broader than a pointed human fingertip.

Avoid:
- spindly fingers;
- sausage fingers;
- uniform cylinders.

#### 4.2 Joint naturalization
Keep MCP/PIP/DIP positions readable, but remove the "beaded" appearance.

Replace rounded collar-like joint transitions with:
- broader joint planes;
- slight asymmetric widening;
- flatter dorsal transitions;
- soft palmar hinge/crease logic;
- continuity into adjacent phalanges.

The joint must read as a joint **inside flesh**, not as a separate ring wrapped around a tube.

Do not erase articulation.

Do not return to v003 softness.

The target is:
**structured but organic**.

#### 4.3 Dorsal and palmar planes
Improve:
- dorsal knuckle plane continuity;
- metacarpal-to-finger transition;
- palmar pad mass;
- proximal finger base mass;
- subtle flattening on load-bearing surfaces.

Avoid perfectly round cross-sections.

Each finger should have a believable:
- dorsal plane;
- lateral plane;
- palmar pad side.

#### 4.4 Web spaces
Preserve distinct finger roots but soften over-deep or mechanically clean interdigital valleys.

Required:
- natural soft-tissue webs;
- variable depth;
- no trench-like cuts;
- no scalloped mitten;
- no fused roots.

The transition from palm to finger root should feel grown, not cut.
