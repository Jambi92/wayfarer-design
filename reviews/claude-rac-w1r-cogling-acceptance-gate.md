## 2. Method point: joint readings at 91 cm (needs author ruling)

The accepted joint-gracility rows (Fenn, Aelari, Vael) use joint breadth ÷ segment length, measured inside a fixed ±1 cm slab. Two things bias that reading for Cogling:

1. **Per-segment ratios are inflated by design.** Cogling's humerus and femur are shortened on purpose, so elbow ÷ humerus reads +17 % over MF even on CGJ7. Canon asks for a *height-normalized* comparison, so the scored rows use joint breadth ÷ stature.
2. **A fixed 1 cm slab is relatively ~1.9× deeper at 91 cm than at 173 cm.** At the wrist it picks up hand, and at the ankle it picks up heel and foot. With the slab scaled to stature (1 cm × stature ÷ 173.14; MF unchanged), the as-built wrist goes from +12.5 % over MF to −4.1 %.

| Joint ÷ stature, CGJ7 vs MF | Fixed slab | Scaled slab |
|---|---|---|
| Elbow | −1.8 % | −2.7 % |
| Wrist | +11.4 % | −6.6 % |
| Knee | −15.4 % | −13.2 % |
| Ankle | +6.2 % | −5.2 % |

Under the fixed slab, even the strongest probe (CGJ3: wrist and ankle targets at the generator maximum, forearm ×0.88) leaves the wrist +3.8 % and the ankle +5.4 % over MF. Bodies at Pipkin and Durrim stature show the same effect.

**Same answer at minimum composition** (muscle 0 / weight 0, scaled slab; tables §1). CGJ7 vs MF: elbow −4.7 %, wrist −8.1 %, knee −17 %, ankle −5.2 %. CGJ7 vs PK: −4.5 / −6.1 / −17 / −9.7 %. So the result is skeletal, not tissue.

**For the author:** accept the scaled-slab, height-normalized reading for Cogling's joint rows (and for later small bodies), or keep the fixed slab. Under the fixed slab, Cogling cannot pass its fine-joint canon with any bounded write.
