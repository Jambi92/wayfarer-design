## 1. Result

S-D3 rejects tiny scaled globes. It asks for ordinary adult-human-compatible globes that physically fit the authored PK/CG craniofacial architecture, and asks for a flag before any head canon changes if they don't fit.

**They do not fit.** The generator socket is close to isometric with the head: capacity ÷ HH is 0.106–0.114 across every body tested. "Capacity" is the largest centred globe that touches no skin vertex.

| Body | HH (cm) | Socket capacity (cm) | Skin vertices inside a centred 2.2 / 2.3 / 2.4 cm globe | Inter-ocular distance (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MF-M-R | 22.51 | 2.45 | 0 / 0 / 0 | 5.86 |
| PK-NAT | 16.20 | 1.76 | 172 / 277 / 362 | 4.20 |
| CG-NAT | 11.81 | 1.26 | 549 / 566 / 574 | 3.01 |
| DU-NAT | 19.46 | 2.17 | 0 / 6 / 11 | 5.18 |

**PK.** A 2.2–2.3 cm adult globe needs a socket about **1.25–1.30 ×** the MF socket, relative to head height. Basis: (globe ÷ HH) ÷ MF capacity ÷ HH (0.109).

**CG.** It needs about **1.71–1.79 ×**. Two 2.3 cm globes centred 3.01 cm apart leave 0.71 cm between them, so the inter-orbital region would also have to widen.

**DU (not in the order; found by the same test).** A 2.2 cm globe fits. A 2.3 cm globe touches 6 skin vertices. The current DU landmark globe is 2.12 cm. This is a small conflict: S-D3 read absolutely also applies to Durrim.

**GR and GO (consequence).** These landmark globes come from the W1c orbit-scaling rule (D-4c): 2.77 and 2.98 cm, which is above the 2.2–2.4 cm ordinary-adult values used here. If S-D3 is an absolute biological rule, GR and GO need ordinary adult globes as well. Those would fit, since their sockets are 2.86 and 3.08 cm.

## 2. Why this is flagged rather than fixed

Fitting adult globes in PK and CG means enlarging the orbits relative to the head. That is the juvenile "huge eyes" direction:
- PIPKIN L33 rules out "huge eyes".
- COGLING L1691 says eyes "remain broadly adult humanoid in scale" and that camera/readability problems "must be solved without enlarging biological features".

**Claude's reading** is that, at the authored head sizes, S-D3's absolute adult globe and the anti-juvenile eye statements pull against each other. The authored head sizes are the CG head of roughly 11–13 cm (RM-SR-04) and the accepted +16 % PK head share. This is a judgement, not a measurement. A moderate allometric orbit (about 1.25 × for PK) could be argued to parallel the already-accepted allometric PK head.

**Head canon was not altered (S-D3).** PK-NAT and CG-NAT keep socket-derived **placeholder** landmark globes (1.72 and 1.23 cm). These are **not biological eye canon**, and the PK/CG eye and orbit readings stay **blocked**.

## 3. Author decisions needed

**G-S1 (PK, CG).** Choose one:
- **(a)** Allow race-authored **miniaturized globe anatomy**. S-D3 allows this only if a race spec explicitly authors it.
- **(b)** Allow orbit/socket size **relative to the head** to rise above MF by a stated maximum, as an allometric exception. Adulthood would then be carried by other cues.
- **(c)** Enlarge the PK/CG **head** until adult globes fit. This touches the accepted S-D2/S-D4 head factors and the anti-oversized-head rule (PIPKIN L33).
- **(d)** Keep PK/CG eye items blocked for W1.

**G-S2 (DU, GR, GO).** Should S-D3's absolute reading also replace the W1c orbit-scaled landmark globes? That would mean DU 2.12 → a fitting adult value, and GR/GO 2.77/2.98 → ordinary adult values.

Claude recommends no option, because each choice changes identity-relevant canon or accepted method.
