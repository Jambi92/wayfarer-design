# 7. GORRUND CANON — WHAT THE FREE SCULPT MUST EXPRESS

The new body must visibly embody the locked Gorrund identity:

> **Massive load-bearing non-human architecture, high absolute structural scale, broad/deep axial organization, substantial joints, and strong axial-to-limb integration.**

It must remain:

- upright;
- plantigrade;
- humanoid;
- adult;
- five-digit;
- capable of human-compatible tool use;
- structurally massive independent of current muscle/fat.

It must not become:

- an enlarged human;
- a bigger Skarn;
- a thick Grask;
- a Durrim scaled upward;
- an ogre/troll caricature;
- a bodybuilder;
- an obese giant;
- a monster with armor-knob joints.

---

# 8. SHAPE THE WHOLE LOAD-BEARING SYSTEM, NOT A TRUNK GIMMICK

Design the organism as one coherent structural chain:

**neck root → shoulder girdle → thorax → lower axial trunk → pelvis → proximal femur → knee → shank → ankle → foot**

and:

**shoulder → upper arm → elbow → forearm → wrist → hand**

The identity must not come primarily from:

- one waist shape;
- one chest plate;
- one giant joint;
- one silhouette trick.

Distribute the identity throughout the body.

---

# 9. AXIAL DESIGN TARGET

The thorax should be:

- broad;
- deep;
- three-dimensional;
- visibly load-bearing;
- anatomically radiused.

It must not be:

- square;
- rectangular;
- slab-sided;
- cylindrical/barrel;
- a uniformly enlarged human rib cage.

The lower axial trunk must:

- carry the thorax into the pelvis;
- permit a natural anatomical minimum where appropriate;
- avoid a pinched human waist;
- avoid the old parallel-column box;
- avoid a soft belly transition.

Let the shape be biologically coherent rather than forcing any prior route profile.

---

# 10. SHOULDER / NECK / GIRDLE

The shoulder girdle must look **carried by the thorax**.

Build:

- substantial clavicular/scapular architecture;

- deltoid-cap identity;

- girdle overhang;

# 11. PELVIS / PROXIMAL FEMUR

The pelvis must visibly function as part of the load path.

- substantial pelvic depth;
- large hip articulation;
- strong pelvis→femoral-head/neck relationship;
- structurally supported proximal femur;

- giant soft buttocks;

---

# 12. LOWER-LIMB STRUCTURE

- large absolute femoral structural scale;
- substantial but integrated knees;
- coherent tibial/shank structure;

- pillar legs;
- swollen knees;
- knob joints;
- tiny ankles;
- Grask high-calf / long-cord emphasis;
- short cartoon-ogre thighs.
