## 1. Author ruling

The current RP2 v001 technical work is accepted as a useful production baseline, but the **visual species identity is not accepted**.

The owner review found that the neutral, untextured Grask still reads too close to a tall human.

This is now an explicit **author-directed Grask identity correction pass**.

Do not preserve the current E3/v001 morphology merely because it previously passed Stage C if that morphology conflicts with this updated owner visual ruling.

The established high-level Grask direction remains:

- troll-like, not generic orc;
- tall and rangy;
- long-limbed;
- upright;
- structurally powerful without bodybuilder bulk;
- clearly non-human in neutral untextured form;
- distinct from Skarn, Marchfolk, Aelari, Gorrund and generic human anatomy.

## 2. Execution policy

Proceed autonomously.

**Do not turn intermediate failures, exploratory candidates, or partial corrections back to Tyler.**

Claude must:

1. diagnose the current identity weaknesses;
2. build corrected production candidates;
3. run its own technical and visual gates;
4. reject failures internally;
5. repair and iterate;
6. complete the full build;
7. return only after a technically passing candidate also passes Claude's own Grask identity review.

Only stop early for a true blocker:
- unresolved canon contradiction that cannot be resolved from this order and existing authority;
- irreconcilable conflict between required species identity and production topology;
- tool/file corruption preventing execution.

Routine topology, shading, deformation, proportion, or identity misses are not owner-review events. Fix them internally.

## 3. Core acceptance target

The final neutral production mesh must visibly read as:

> **a distinct Grask troll-like humanoid, not a stretched human, not a heavy Aelari, not a Skarn variant, and not a generic fantasy orc brute.**

The result must pass even with:
- neutral gray material;
- no skin pigmentation;
- no hair;
- no facial hair;
- no clothing;
- no scars;
- no culture cues;
- neutral expression;
- neutral pose.

Texture and later surface detail may enhance identity, but must not be required to create it.

## 4. Preserve the successful foundation

Retain the useful Grask architecture unless a local relationship must be adjusted to improve identity:

- 198–239 cm accepted stature envelope;
- approximately 218 cm reference target;
- long-limb / short-torso organization;
- strong forearm contribution;
- strong lower-leg contribution;
- substantial anatomical reach;
- upright plantigrade stance;
- moderate-to-substantial but non-brute shoulder structure;
- narrow/deep Grask trunk logic rather than Durrim compact breadth;
- current functional five-digit hands and feet;
- no required hunch;
- no required claws;
- no required tusks;
- no mandatory giant underbite;
- no bodybuilder-orc proportions.

Do not convert Grask into a Warcraft-style broad brute.

## 5. Required identity corrections

### A. Head and face — mandatory

The current head reads too human.

Strengthen the underlying Grask craniofacial architecture.

Required direction:

- greater vertical facial organization;
- stronger integrated midface identity;
- clearer relationship between orbit, zygomatic region and long midface;
- more substantial lower-face / mandible contribution without a giant square jaw;
- less generic human craniofacial read;
- maintain intelligent humanoid cranium;
- maintain non-muzzle, non-ape-like projection;
- do not rely on giant nose, scowl, tiny eyes, or monster caricature.

The result should visibly carry the written Grask concept of:
- elongated but structurally grounded face;
- greater facial vertical contribution;
- long integrated midface;
- distinct orbital/zygomatic organization;
- substantial vertically organized mandible.

### B. Ears — mandatory

The current ears read too human and are not carrying enough species identity.

Strengthen Grask ear morphology so it is recognizably non-human while remaining biologically coherent and compatible with the established folded / late-tapering Grask direction.

Requirements:
- clearly different from Marchfolk/human ear silhouette;
- distinct folded internal architecture;
- late taper rather than a generic pointed elf ear;
- no giant caricature ears;
- no simple Aelari/Fenn ear reuse;
- visible species read in front, profile and three-quarter views.

Treat ear identity as a major Grask carrier.

### C. Front-view body identity — mandatory

The current front view still reads too close to a tall human.

Improve the species read through coordinated skeletal relationships, not superficial bulk.

Focus on:

- girdle / shoulder architecture;
- clavicle/scapula-to-arm origin relationship;
- long-arm-to-torso organization;
- torso breadth/depth relationship;
- waist and ribcage organization;
- pelvis/hip relationship;
- visible joint structure;
- lower-leg participation in the silhouette.

The front view must stop reading as "tall human with stretched limbs."

Do not solve this by:
- increasing muscle globally;
- widening shoulders into Skarn;
- creating a V-shaped bodybuilder torso;
- narrowing the waist into monster anatomy;
- adding permanent hunch.

### D. Hands — mandatory

Current hands are too human in shape.

Keep:
- five digits;
- ordinary nails;
- fine manipulation;
- no claws;
- no knuckle-dragging.

Strengthen Grask hand identity through anatomy:
- larger absolute hand read;
- proportionally stronger finger-to-palm relationship where valid;
- more distinctive metacarpal / knuckle organization;
- less generic human palm/finger silhouette;
- preserve functionality and weapon/tool use.

Do not exaggerate finger length into horror/alien anatomy.

### E. Feet — mandatory

Current feet are too human.

Keep:
- five digits;
- plantigrade locomotion;
- ordinary nails;
- no claws;
- normal footwear compatibility.

Strengthen Grask foot identity through:
- more species-specific forefoot/heel proportions;
- toe relationship;
- midfoot/ankle integration;
- broader or narrower local structure only where coherent with the accepted frame;
- less "human foot scaled to a tall body" read.

Do not make them paw-like or digitigrade.

## 8. Relationship-aware body correction

Do not modify isolated proportions blindly.

Every body edit must preserve coherent Grask relationships:

- limb length vs torso;
- arm span vs stature;
- forearm vs upper arm;
- lower leg vs femur;
- shoulder breadth vs stature;
- pelvis vs leg architecture;
- hand size vs forearm;
- foot size vs lower leg;
- head/neck vs stature.

Avoid a "Frankenstein" correction where each region is more exotic but the whole no longer feels biologically coherent.
