# Audit: Gorrund Character Design v1.0 (first pass complete)

Auditor: Claude. These are notes to check, not changes. Nothing here overrides the spec in `races/10-gorrund.md`; Tyler decides what, if anything, changes.

## Open notes

- **Part 1 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** Skeletal massiveness is kept apart from muscle and fat, "load-bearing" carries no gameplay bonus or speed implication, size never implies intelligence or tempo, the legacy swim and sneak traits stay gameplay-only (consistent with the Grask stealth housekeeping rule), reach stays four separate concepts, equipment never scales, world constraints never shrink the anatomy, and the Skarn, Grask and Durrim identity statements match their tabs. The earlier Grask shorthand ("thin Gorrund," "Gorrund assumptions") happens to point the same way as this design, but under the Grask clarifications it was never prior approval; Gorrund is being defined here on its own.
- **Durrim convergence risk (the main thing to settle in Part 2).** Durrim's approved proportional signature is a broad, deep, compact torso; substantial joints relative to stature; lower limb contribution to height than Marchfolk; strong neck-torso integration; and large hands and feet relative to stature. Gorrund Part 1 describes broad and deep thorax (§14–15, with depth "relative to stature"), substantial joints (§41), lower limb contribution and greater axial contribution (§27) and strong neck-torso integration (§20). Scaled up, that reads very close to Durrim's recipe. §21 and §86 forbid "Durrim scaled up" but don't yet say which proportional relationship differs. The key question for Part 2 is whether Gorrund limb contribution is lower than Marchfolk and Skarn (Durrim-like) or only lower than Grask; §27 compares only with Grask.
- **Comparison targets unnamed (terminology).** §14 says thoracic breadth trends high "relative to relevant comparison populations" and §15 says depth is high "relative to stature and breadth" without naming a population, while §13 and §27 compare only with Grask. Under the project rule, Part 2 would benefit from naming the reference (Marchfolk, Skarn, Grask).
- **"Greater structural scale" at equal height (same issue as Grask–Fenn).** In the Skarn test at 208–229 cm, both have the same stature, so Gorrund's "greater structural scale" presumably means greater skeletal breadth, depth, joint and bone cross-sectional dimensions at matched height, not greater overall size.
- **Overlap list.** Checked against approved ranges: Gorrund 208–251 overlaps Skarn (208–229), Grask (208–239), Aelari (208–221), **Fenn (208–211)**, Sagekin (208 only) and Halvren's central envelope (208–213, plus valid tails); Marchfolk (to 203) and Vael (to 203) don't overlap. Part 1 tests Skarn, Grask, Aelari, Sagekin and Halvren but not Fenn, whose narrow 208–211 cm window would be a boundary test like the Grask–Vael one.
- **Sagekin "test" is a single point.** Sagekin max and Gorrund min are both about 208 cm, so the shared stature is one value rather than a range; the equal-height rule still allows it, but it's the thinnest possible boundary test.
- **World ceiling.** If 251 cm survives, it supersedes Grask's 239 cm as the current world-validation upper stature; the Grask record already said 239 cm wasn't a permanent maximum, so nothing there needs changing.
- **Current game compared with Part 1 (not a change).** I haven't re-read the prototype Gorrund class values; any scale, stats or trait text stay prototype and non-authoritative under the authority rule.

* **Part 2 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** Breadth never becomes muscle and depth never becomes obesity (§9, GRR-BODY-16), axial emphasis never means cartoon-short limbs (§3, §23, §25), depth never becomes a barrel chest (§8, §10), large hands keep full dexterity and feet stay non-monstrous, frame correlations follow the Grask-era soft-correlation rule, no numerical ratios are implied, and "load-bearing" creates no stats (§58, §64, §97). The Skarn row's "greater natural muscle-volume potential already approved" matches the Skarn spec's "more natural muscle volume" as resolved in the Halvren patch (a shifted capacity or distribution, not a default amount). Five digits per hand and foot resolves the Part 1 expectation.
* **Durrim convergence still only partly answered (carry forward).** Part 2 says the same recipe is never reused (§101) and a minimum-height broad Gorrund mustn't look "Durrim-like in proportion" (§68), but every proportional comparison of limbs and torso is still against Grask only (§4, §23, §31). Durrim's approved signature is lower limb contribution than Marchfolk, broad and deep torso, substantial joints relative to stature and large hands and feet; Gorrund Part 2 adds thoracic depth "relative to stature" (§8) and palms broad and deep relative to hand length (§42). So the question from Part 1 stands: are Gorrund limbs proportionally shorter than Marchfolk and Skarn (Durrim-like), about human-typical, or longer? Even a statement that Gorrund limb contribution is roughly human-typical (lower only than Grask) would give the Durrim test a positive anchor. Skarn are already "slightly more torso-dominant than Marchfolk," so Gorrund vs Skarn torso share also needs a direction for the Skarn test.
* **Boundary labels (minor).** The Aelari test at 208–221 cm sits at the bottom of Gorrund's range and the top of Aelari's, and the Sagekin test at 208 cm is a single boundary point; under the equal-height rule both would carry the **Cross-Population Equal-Height Boundary Test** label (Part 1 already labeled the Sagekin one).
* **Original brief vs Part 1–2 (not a change).** The brief's race table describes Gorrund with "long arms"; Parts 1–2 approve large absolute but not reach-dominant arms, with greater relative arm length for Grask. The approved spec wins; the brief's summary wording is worth updating in the final terminology review.
* **Fenn overlap.** Part 2's tests still don't name Fenn (overlap about 208–211 cm), carried from the Part 1 note and the OPEN register row.

- **Boundary and arm clarification: resolves the boundary-label, brief "long arms" and Fenn notes above.** No contradiction introduced; the Fenn description (gracile, extremity-emphasized, narrower joints, lower structural mass) matches the Fenn tab and the Grask–Fenn wording. The Durrim-convergence note isn't addressed by this patch and stays OPEN in the register: the arm wording now anchors Gorrund arms as not unusual relative to height, but limb and torso contribution relative to Marchfolk and Skarn is still unstated.

* **Part 3 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** Breadth isn't the sole identifier (GOR-FACE-02, 14, 15), depth never becomes prognathism, the mandible and brow aren't mandatory, tiny eyes and tusks aren't required, no intelligence or ugliness coding survives, correlations use envelopes rather than hard locks, face controls are provisional per the project-wide classification, FD domain names are correct, and the Vael ear test avoids the base-and-attachment trap the Grask ear clarification fixed.
* **Durrim craniofacial convergence (same issue as the body, now in the face).** Durrim Part 3 approved a compact cranium with greater cranial breadth relative to cranial height than Marchfolk (tendency), greater craniofacial depth relative to facial height, substantial midface integration, substantial mandibular presence and strong head-neck-body integration as a major identifier. Gorrund Part 3 describes substantial cranial breadth, meaningful craniofacial depth, a deep integrated midface, a substantial mandible, less dependence on vertical elongation and strong head-neck-body integration. §40 separates them by compactness and short-stature integration versus large scale and tall-body integration, which is scale and context rather than a craniofacial relationship that differs. A face-level anchor (for example a Gorrund relationship Durrim lack, or one Durrim have that Gorrund don't) would give the Durrim face test (§113) something positive to check. Logged OPEN with the body-proportion question.
* **Ear convergence with humans and Durrim (ambiguity).** Gorrund ears are broad, deep, strongly attached, rounded-to-angular and short-to-moderate, with a lobe. That clearly separates them from elves and Grask, but ordinary human ears are also rounded, and Durrim ears are "broadly humanoid," non-pointed and variable in breadth and projection. §67 says structural breadth, depth and cartilage relationships must create a non-human identity but, like the first Grask ear draft, doesn't yet say which cartilage relationship differs from a large human ear. Naming one (for example helix or antihelix form, concha depth, the upper-contour angle, or how the ear sits on the broad cranium) would give GOR-EAR-01 to 06 and the human ear test a positive target, as the Grask ear clarification did.
* **Comparison target (terminology).** §6 sets cranial breadth "relative to their overall head architecture," which is relative to itself; Durrim's equivalent names Marchfolk and cranial height. Naming a base (cranial height or length, versus Marchfolk or Skarn) would follow the project rule.
* **Tusk-like canines.** §28 says the Gorrund answer needn't match Grask; both stay OPEN in the register as separate items.

- **Craniofacial and ear clarification: resolves the Part 3 Durrim face, ear and skull-breadth notes.** No new contradiction. Durrim anatomy and all elven and Grask ear rows are untouched; the Gorrund ear now has a positive target (deep bowl, strong antihelical folds, broad continuous rim, close-set broad attachment) that none of the human, Durrim, elven or Grask ear descriptions uses. Two small observations, not conflicts: Gorrund's corrected skull-breadth tendency (greater breadth relative to height than Marchfolk) now reads almost the same as Durrim's, which is fine because §20 moves the distinction to the surrounding transverse organization; and Grask ears also have "robust, visibly folded cartilage," so the Grask–Gorrund ear distinction rests on taper and silhouette as §31 says, not on folding alone.
- **Durrim body-proportion question still OPEN.** This patch settles the face; the body-level question from Parts 1–2 (Gorrund limb and torso contribution relative to Marchfolk and Skarn) stays in the register. The Transverse Structural Continuity idea may offer a model for it, but no body relationship has been named yet.

* **Part 4 audit: no contradiction with earlier Gorrund parts, clarifications or cross-race rules.** Skin never classifies the race, the Grask and Vael distinctions are carried by anatomy (and now name the transverse face and ear anatomy), no green or stone-gray default, no thick skin from size, rough skin, baldness and grime aren't required, eyes stay biological, aging doesn't add caricature, phenotype packages are banned, and the flat-light transverse-architecture test (§90) usefully ties Part 4 back to the Part 3 clarification.
* **Pigmentation envelope has no lighter complexions (worth a deliberate decision).** Every Gorrund skin family in §3 is a brown, umber, ochre, olive, taupe or earth-gray-brown, and the hair envelope is dark-to-muted too (bright blond OPEN). By comparison, Durrim Part 4 approved "lighter, intermediate and darker complexions," Marchfolk run from very light to deep brown, and the elves include pale tones. That makes Gorrund the only population whose natural envelope is confined to human-observed brown and dark tones, and they're also the large "ogre" race the spec works hard to keep away from brute stereotypes (§94). Players could read a large, strong, dark-only race as echoing real-world racial caricature, even though nothing in the spec intends that. It isn't a contradiction, since §4 says brown isn't required and §2 bans exclusive palettes, but the envelope as written works like a de facto exclusion of light skin. Options if you want to address it: include lighter and intermediate earth-toned complexions in the envelope as Durrim did, or keep the center earth-toned but state explicitly that lighter complexions are valid with frequency OPEN. Logged OPEN; not changed.

Durrim's approved body recipe is a broad, deep torso, substantial joints relative to stature, lower limb contribution than Marchfolk and large hands and feet relative to stature; Gorrund's is broad and deep axial construction, substantial joints, greater torso contribution than Grask, and large hands and feet. A body relationship Durrim don't share, for example stating Gorrund limb contribution relative to Marchfolk and Skarn, or a body analogue of the transverse-continuity idea (such as shoulder-girdle-to-pelvis continuity), would let a matched-scale body silhouette test pass on something other than size, the way the face test now does.
