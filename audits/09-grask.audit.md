Auditor: Claude. These are notes to check, not changes. Nothing here overrides the spec in `races/09-grask.md`; Tyler decides what, if anything, changes.

## Open notes

- **Part 1 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** It keeps the universal rules (frame versus composition, capacity versus current muscularity, absolute versus proportional size, validity versus frequency, no uniform scaling, culture separate from biology), the Skarn and Durrim identity statements match their tabs, the reach separation matches Durrim Part 5, the legacy traits follow the Durrim pattern, and no facial-control structure is proposed.
- **Overlap list: resolved by the comparative anatomy clarification**, which adds Fenn, Vael and Halvren and labels the boundary tests.
- **Arm contribution wording: resolved by the clarification**, which locks greater arm length and span relative to standing height, not just relative to torso.
- **Neck comparison target: resolved by the clarification** (Grask–Aelari neck distinction and failure condition).
- **Gorrund references: resolved by the clarification** (future comparative placeholders only).
- **Tallest population so far: recorded by the clarification** as the current world-validation ceiling, not a permanent maximum.
- **Grask–Fenn convergence risk (clarification check, not a change).** Fenn's approved identifiers already include longer arms relative to torso, a somewhat greater forearm share, longer hands and fingers, and forearm and lower-leg proportions read at medium distance. Grask's candidate identifiers (forearm emphasis, lower-leg elongation, possibly longer fingers) point the same way, so the 198–211 cm Grask–Fenn test is where these traits alone won't separate them. The distinction has to come from the rest of the system (joint and base dimensions, torso and pelvis, thoracic architecture, gracility versus structural presence), which Part 2 should make explicit.
- **"Greater overall structural scale" at equal height (ambiguity).** In the Fenn row, a Grask and a Fenn at the same stature can't differ in overall scale in the absolute sense of height, so this presumably means greater skeletal robustness and structural presence relative to stature (joints, bones, torso). Worth wording that way in Part 2 under the absolute versus relative rule.
- **Current game compared with Part 1 (not a change).** I didn't re-read the prototype Grask class values. Scale, stats, trait and flavor text are PROTOTYPE / NON-AUTHORITATIVE under the clarification §21.

* **Part 2 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** It matches Part 1 and the clarification (arm length and span relative to height, torso contribution relative to stature, neck left out of the elongation signature), follows the universal frame-versus-composition and capacity rules, and follows Durrim's interaction and equipment principles (canonical weapons, no world or furniture scaling, no gameplay from anatomy). The Sagekin comparison matches Sagekin's approved "slightly longer-limbed average." Five digits per hand and foot resolves the Part 1 OPEN digit-count row.
* **Grask–Fenn distinction now carried by the right traits.** §67 answers the earlier convergence note: Fenn's forearm share, long hands and fingers overlap Grask's new forearm and finger tendencies, so the separation rests on larger absolute structure (joints and bones at the same height), a different torso and pelvis system, and reach organization. The hand and foot cross-race tests (§72–73) list Marchfolk, Skarn, Aelari and Grask but not Fenn; since hands and fingers are where Grask and Fenn overlap most, Fenn may be worth adding.
* **Upper-arm wording (terminology).** §24 says upper arms have "increased proportional contribution compared with human reference anatomy" without naming the base (stature or total arm length). §25 and the clarification §13 name both for the forearm, so Part 2's own §2 rule suggests naming it here too.
* **Gorrund mentions (not a spec).** §47 ("Gorrund assumptions") and §57 ("an accidental Gorrund prototype") read as though Gorrund are large, muscular and heavy. Under the clarification §20 these stay placeholders and set no Gorrund anatomy.
* **Validity versus frequency.** §41's "Narrow-frame Grask may trend toward somewhat narrower feet" is a soft correlation, consistent with §45; it should stay a tendency in randomization and presets, never a frame lock.

- **Part 2 minor clarification: resolves the four Part 2 notes above** (Fenn added to hand and foot tests, upper-arm base named, Gorrund shorthand neutralized, frame-to-foot link made a soft correlation). No contradiction introduced. The new general correlation rule matches how earlier races already treat soft tendencies (for example Durrim frame and composition, and Halvren's no-fixed-phenotype-package rule).

* **Part 3 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** It keeps structural anatomy separate from soft tissue, expression, presentation and lighting; uses the FD domain names correctly; classifies control organization as provisional and lists coverage rather than UI; matches the clarification's moderate-to-long neck; keeps the Durrim contrast in line with the Durrim depth tendency; and treats hair and beard as zero required recognition, like Durrim. No "troll = ugly," prognathism, tusk or stretched-human-face assumption survives.
* **Ear convergence risk (ambiguity worth a patch).** The Elf Comparative Review already gives elves every trait Part 3 uses for Grask ears: Aelari ears have upward and backward orientation, moderate-to-long length and a gradual taper; Vael have a "somewhat broader base," more lateral and backward orientation and strong skull-attachment integration; Fenn have greater outward and backward projection. Grask ears (§39–45) are elongated, broader-based, backward or upward tapering, more substantially attached, moderately to strongly elongated, and vary across upward, backward and lateral sweep. §44 compares the base with "the gracile elven ear foundation," but Vael are already the broader-based elf. Part 3 says Grask ears must differ in base, cartilage, attachment, orientation and silhouette, but doesn't yet say how, so GR-EAR-01 to 04 have no positive non-elven target. A short clarification naming what makes a Grask ear non-elven (for example cartilage form, helix and upper-ear structure, where the taper starts, lobe, or how the ear sits on the longer Grask cranium) would help before Part 4.
* **"Long-planed" (terminology).** The Aelari row describes Grask as "more strongly long-planed facial architecture," a term not defined elsewhere; it presumably means greater facial vertical contribution with vertically organized zygomatic and mandibular planes.
* **Facial height base (terminology).** §4 measures facial vertical contribution relative to facial breadth, which is partly a width ratio, while §34 says narrowness isn't the identifier. For broad-faced Grask to stay Grask, verticality presumably also needs a base independent of breadth (relative to cranial height or head size), and §13's midface contribution presumably means relative to total facial height. Worth naming the base under the project's terminology rule.
* **Dentition wording (small assumption).** §20's "ordinary omnivorous-style functional use" implies a dietary biology no spec has set yet. It's hedged ("unless later biological design establishes otherwise") and harmless as a baseline, but diet itself isn't approved anywhere.
* **Aging and lifecycle.** Part 3 defers aging to Part 4 and keeps lifecycle OPEN, consistent with how Durrim and the elves handled it.

- **Ear and craniofacial clarification: resolves the Part 3 ear, "long-planed," facial-height and dentition notes.** The Part 3 text now points to the clarification wherever it used the superseded wording (§39 lock, §44 base comparison, the Aelari row, the dentition quote and the status summary). No contradiction introduced. The new ear architecture doesn't touch the elven ear rows in the Elf Comparative Review; it defines Grask by cartilage, upper-ear body, taper timing and lobe, which none of the three elf rows describes, so the Grask–Vael and Grask–Aelari tests now have a positive target.

* **Part 4 audit: no contradiction needing immediate correction.** Green is never mandatory, no habitat is set, color labels are marked observational rather than genetic, skin texture avoids monster coding, hair, skin and iris stay free of fixed packages, eyes establish no low-light or aquatic biology, age sets no lifespan, environment and magic stay separate from biology, and the three age concepts and lighting invariance match Durrim Part 4 and the universal rules. Ear translucency (§13) fits the clarified thin terminal ear region.
* **"Distinguishable from human pigmentation" is population-level only (ambiguity).** Marchfolk's approved range already includes olive, tan and bronze, brown and deep brown, so individual warm-umber, ochre-brown, clay-brown and olive-brown Grask can match an individual human's skin. That's fine under §1, §7 and the brown-skin test, but §3 reads best as "the Grask envelope as a whole differs from human pigmentation," not "every Grask skin tone is non-human." The distinction presumably lives in the envelope's shape (the moss, gray-green and gray-olive undertone range humans don't reach) rather than in any single tone.
* **Gray tones overlap Vael (check, not a change).** Vael's approved family includes cool gray, neutral gray, warmer gray, ash-brown and desaturated brown, which sits next to Grask gray-olive and cool stone-gray-brown. The gray-skin recognition test (§63) checks against undead, stone and diseased humans; at the 198–203 cm boundary a gray Grask and a gray Vael could share tone, so anatomy has to carry that comparison too (already required by the Grask–Vael boundary test).
* **§81 wording (minor).** "No race-specific pigmentation mismatches between body regions" presumably means no unintended mismatch between separate face, body and hand assets, not a ban on the natural regional variation §11 supports.
* **White hair.** §24 excludes bright white as ordinary adult pigmentation, while §27–28 allow age whitening and possibly premature or naturally lighter hair; these fit together as age or individual variation versus a population color.
