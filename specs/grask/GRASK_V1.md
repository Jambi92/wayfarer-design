# Grask Character Design v1.0 (first pass complete)

This is the Grask (Troll) specification, a distinct tall humanoid population. It's design only, with no UE5 changes. v1.0 arrived in five parts, all complete: Part 1 (biological foundation, stature and rangy skeletal architecture, with its comparative anatomy clarification), Part 2 (detailed body proportions, hands, feet and composition architecture, with its minor consistency clarification), Part 3 (craniofacial anatomy, ears, hair and facial-hair biology, with its ear and craniofacial clarification), Part 4 (pigmentation, skin, hair color, eyes, aging and environmental appearance, with its pigmentation overlap clarification) and Part 5 (movement, animation, equipment, world interaction and final first-pass review). The final audit passed, so **Grask Character Design v1.0 is FIRST-PASS COMPLETE.** That doesn't resolve every open question, finalize gameplay or technical architecture, or authorize UE5 implementation.

# Part 1 Biological foundation, stature and rangy skeletal architecture

## 1–2. Core identity and silhouette

> **Grask are a distinct tall humanoid population defined by elongated, rangy skeletal architecture, substantial functional reach, a relatively lean structural silhouette, and body proportions that remain recognizably non-human even when composition, face, hair, clothing and cultural presentation are neutralized.**

Grask aren't humans enlarged vertically, Skarn with troll faces, thin Gorrund, or monsters whose anatomy exists only to look threatening. The silhouette emphasizes tall adult stature, long limb contribution and functional reach, a compact-to-moderate torso contribution compared with limb length, distinct shoulder and pelvic architecture, long forearms and lower legs as meaningful contributors, large hands and feet suited to the skeleton, and a visually high but anatomically coherent limb-to-torso ratio. It avoids gorilla caricature, ape posture, a hunched monster silhouette, a giant human silhouette, an extremely narrow stick figure and a bodybuilder troll.

## 3–5. Stature and adult read

| Minimum | Reference | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| about 198 cm (6'6") | about 218 cm (7'2") | about 239 cm (7'10") |

This is a **FIRST-PASS BIOLOGICAL RANGE — SUBJECT TO CROSS-RACE VALIDATION**. It intentionally overlaps upper Marchfolk, Skarn, upper Sagekin and upper Aelari, and equal-height characters from those populations must stay anatomically distinct. A 239 cm Grask is never a 198 cm Grask uniformly enlarged: height distributes through valid changes in lower and upper limb lengths, torso, pelvis, spine, neck and head contribution, with combined-proportion validity mandatory. Minimum-height Grask still read as adult Grask, never with juvenile proportions, an oversized childlike head, short immature limbs or childlike facial architecture; **shorter-for-Grask doesn't mean young.**

## 6–7. Skeletal identity and structural presence

> **Elongated skeletal leverage and reach rather than compact structural mass.**

| Population | Skeletal direction |
| --- | --- |
| Skarn | Large-scale powerful human anatomy |
| Durrim | Compact structural power and high skeletal presence relative to stature |
| Grask | Tall rangy anatomy with strongly expressed limb contribution and reach |

"Rangy" never means fragile, weak, tiny-boned, starved or emaciated. Grask skeletons support their stature and locomotion, and bones and joints may be substantial in absolute terms while looking relatively lean compared with total height, so **absolute structural size** and **structural size relative to stature** always stay distinct.

## 34–45. Frame and composition

Grask use the universal Narrow, Balanced and Broad skeletal-frame concept, operating inside Grask anatomy rather than human presets on a tall mesh. **Narrow** reduces skeletal breadth, torso and shoulder and pelvic breadth while keeping Grask limb architecture, stature relationships, joint validity and skeletal identity (narrow isn't fragile). **Balanced** is a convenient central starting point, not canonical, ideal or most correct. **Broad** may increase thoracic, shoulder and pelvic skeletal breadth and joint and base dimensions, but isn't Skarn, muscular, fat or maximum everything. Frame stays independent of height, muscularity, body fat, face, personality and culture, supporting combinations such as tall, narrow and muscular; shorter-for-Grask, broad and low-muscle; tall, broad and higher-fat; and reference height, narrow and higher-fat.

Muscularity varies broadly: never universally skinny, sinewy, muscular or weak-looking. A low-muscle Grask stays Grask through skeleton and proportions, and a highly muscular one never becomes Skarn-shaped. **Muscular Development Capacity** (the biological range) stays separate from **Current Muscularity**, and whether Grask capacity differs from humans is **OPEN**, never inferred from silhouette. Body fat ranges from very lean through moderate to higher, and troll biology is never synonymous with gauntness, a pot belly or obesity. Fat amount and distribution stay separate, and soft tissue never erases the long-limbed skeleton. High muscularity with high body fat is valid within biological limits, never forcing thin, muscular or fat categories. Muscle and fat wrap the approved skeleton without redefining it (pectorals don't change thoracic breadth, abdominal fat doesn't change the pelvis, shoulder muscle doesn't move the shoulder joint), and muscle along upper arms, forearms, thighs and lower legs stays believable, never human limbs stretched lengthwise.

## 46–53. Sex, hair, skin, face, culture and personhood

Exact sex-related anatomy is **OPEN**, never assumed identical to humans or exaggerated for readability, and every configuration preserves Grask anatomy; whether average skeletal dimorphism is minimal, moderate or strong is **OPEN**. Scalp, facial and body hair biology come in later parts, never assuming trolls are hairless, very hairy, bald or bearded. Pigmentation, skin surface, texture and environmental appearance come later, and green or gray fantasy skin is never an unexamined default. Detailed craniofacial design is Part 3, but one requirement is locked now:

> **A Grask must eventually remain identifiable as Grask even if the head is visually neutralized during body-silhouette testing.**

Biology is never inferred from fantasy troll culture: Grask aren't biologically primitive, violent, cannibalistic, tribal, unintelligent, solitary, cave-dwelling or swamp-dwelling, which are worldbuilding questions. Intelligence is never encoded through cranial size, posture, facial expression, movement or body shape, and biology doesn't determine intellect unless an explicit biological mechanic is later approved (it isn't now).

> **Grask are people with distinct biology, not monster rigs made playable.**

Their anatomy can be strongly non-human while keeping individuality, neutral posture, fine manipulation, expressive range, clothing compatibility, social animation and customization depth.

## 54–59. Legacy traits, aquatic status and locomotion

"Grask hold their breath about 2× as long" and "Grask swim faster" stay **GAMEPLAY TRAIT — SUBJECT TO LATER REVIEW**. No giant lungs, aquatic ancestry or special blood chemistry is invented to justify breath, and swimming never dictates anatomy before gameplay review; any retained trait's explanation is evaluated separately. **Grask aquatic specialization — NOT ESTABLISHED**: webbed fingers or toes, enlarged lungs, special skin, aquatic eyes, a tail and gill structures aren't approved. Grask are upright plantigrade bipeds capable of normal humanoid locomotion, and long limbs will shape future stride, cadence, reach, turning, crouching and interaction, with performance not finalized.

> **Grask do not require a permanent hunch to look like trolls.**

Individual posture and cultural body language vary, but neutral anatomy supports upright posture. Grask aren't knuckle-walkers, and long arms stay compatible with upright walking, running, tool and weapon use and fine manipulation.

## 10–14. Arm length, span and forearm

> **Grask arms are not merely long relative to their torso. They also trend toward greater arm length and arm span relative to total standing height than Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy.**

This is an important population tendency, so reach identity can't be produced just by shortening the torso. Part 2 distinguishes absolute arm length, arm length relative to height, arm length relative to torso, arm span relative to height, and upper-arm, forearm and hand contribution, and never says "long arms" without the comparison where precision matters. Part 1 §26 is strengthened: Grask trend toward greater **arm span relative to standing height** than Marchfolk and Skarn, with exact ratios **OPEN pending prototype validation** and no invented numbers. The forearm tendency stays provisional: forearm contribution may be proportionally greater relative to total arm length and total stature than in human reference anatomy, with the upper-arm-to-forearm relationship left to Part 2 and no extreme forearm elongation locked. Even with real arm elongation, mandatory anatomy never approaches caricatured ape proportions; Grask stay upright, bipedal, tool-using, fine-manipulating humanoids whose reach fits ordinary social and occupational behavior.

## 15–18. Neck, torso and limb coordination

Both Grask and Aelari may have meaningful neck length, but in different roles. Aelari neck elongation is part of their **vertically distributed elven elongation** through cranial, cervical, torso and limb architecture. Grask neck length supports tall stature, head stabilization, shoulder transition and the rangy body, but isn't meant to be one of the strongest vertical-elongation signals.

> **Grask should not generally rely on extreme neck length for racial identity. Their strongest elongation signal remains limb contribution and reach.**

A neutral Grask at overlapping height that resembles **an Aelari with heavier joints and longer arms**, because both share strongly elongated neck and torso relationships, fails. Grask's lower torso contribution means **torso contribution relative to total stature**, not an absolutely short torso; at the 218 cm reference, a Grask torso may still be large in absolute terms, so absolute torso dimensions and torso contribution to stature stay distinct. Grask identity emerges from coordinated relationships among torso, pelvis, femur, lower leg, shoulder, upper arm, forearm, hands and feet, never by maximizing every limb segment at once, and relationship-aware constraints stay mandatory.

## 19–21. World ceiling, Gorrund and prototype values

This isn't a permanent project maximum, and future races, including Gorrund, may exceed it once designed. Every Grask reference to Gorrund is a **future comparative placeholder only**, setting no Gorrund height, mass, proportions or structure. "Thin Gorrund" is no longer a comparison with a defined race and means only that Grask must have a positive anatomical identity rather than becoming whatever Gorrund turns out to be with reduced composition; the final comparison waits for Gorrund. Any current Grask scale, stats, trait descriptions, environmental descriptions, swamp or cave associations and aquatic flavor text are **PROTOTYPE / NON-AUTHORITATIVE** unless the spec independently approves them, and prototype flavor text never retroactively defines Grask biology or culture.

# Part 2 Detailed body proportions, hands, feet and composition architecture

## 1–3. Core principle, language and signature

> **Grask anatomy must read as a coordinated rangy skeletal system, not a human skeleton with individually stretched limbs.**

No single proportion creates Grask identity; the body stays coherent across torso, spine, shoulders, pelvis, arms, legs, hands, feet and joints. Every comparison distinguishes absolute length, length relative to total stature, length relative to adjacent anatomy, breadth, depth, skeletal structural presence and external circumference, never using "long," "thin," "thick" or "large" loosely where the anatomy matters.

> **Tall stature combined with increased limb contribution to total height, increased arm span relative to height, and coordinated elongation of multiple limb segments without equivalent elongation of every axial body region.**

This signature distinguishes Grask from Aelari's vertically distributed elongation.

## 4–13. Torso, shoulders and pelvis

Compared with Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy, Grask trend toward lower torso contribution to standing height, greater lower-limb contribution, greater upper-limb length relative to height and greater arm span relative to height, as population tendencies, not fixed ratios. A Grask torso can be large in absolute dimensions while contributing proportionally less to height, so **proportionally reduced torso contribution** never means **small torso**.

| Region | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Ribcage | Functional at large body size, with independent skeletal breadth, depth, vertical length, lower-rib relationship and taper; never a human ribcage stretched vertically |
| Thoracic breadth | May be substantial in absolute terms at reference height, but generally reads less broad relative to stature than Skarn, surviving composition neutralization |
| Thoracic depth | Meaningful: never paper-thin for a rangy look, and never approaching Durrim proportional depth |
| Shoulders | Evaluated as absolute skeletal breadth, breadth relative to torso, breadth relative to stature and relationship to arm origin, never one Shoulder Width concept |
| Scapulae and clavicles | Support long functional arms while preserving upright posture, large movement ranges and arms that don't look dislocated, visibly distinct from widened human clavicles; no ape-like shoulder invented to justify reach (exact morphology for later prototyping) |
| Waist and abdomen | Broad variation in skeletal waist relationship, abdominal soft tissue, muscle and fat distribution; no required extremely narrow waist, V-shaped torso or gaunt abdomen |
| Pelvis | Locked functional requirement: **Grask pelvis geometry must coordinate long lower limbs, upright bipedal locomotion and the Grask torso without being created by simply scaling or narrowing a human pelvis.** Exact morphology **OPEN** |
| Pelvic breadth | Supports Narrow, Balanced and Broad frames, with external hip width kept distinct from skeletal pelvic breadth, gluteal muscle and fat distribution |

## 14–20. Legs, knees and ankles

Lower limbs are major stature contributors, trending toward greater leg length relative to height than Marchfolk and Skarn, never defined by one Leg Length slider. Femora trend toward long absolute length and greater contribution to stature than human reference anatomy, coordinated with pelvis, knee, lower leg and muscle distribution. The Part 1 lower-leg direction is now an **approved first-pass population tendency**:

> **Grask lower-leg length is an important contributor to their rangy silhouette and may be proportionally emphasized relative to human reference anatomy.**

Exact ratios are OPEN. There's no single femur-to-lower-leg relationship: valid individuals may lean femur-dominant, balanced or lower-leg-dominant around a coherent distribution, all within combined-proportion validity. Leg elongation fails if it creates stilt-like anatomy, an extremely high crotch, a tiny-looking torso, fragile knees or an unnatural hip transition. Knees are substantial in absolute structure for Grask stature and long lever arms and may look relatively lean against total height (**visually narrow never means structurally weak**). Ankles stay mechanically credible, never uniformly scaled human ankles, tiny joints or oversized monster joints.

## 21–27. Arms, span and reach

> **Grask trend toward greater total arm length relative to standing height than Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy.** This is genuine proportional elongation, not an optical effect of a proportionally smaller torso.

> **Grask trend toward greater arm span relative to standing height than Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy.** Exact ratios stay OPEN pending prototype measurement and comparative validation.

Arm span emerges from shoulder architecture, upper-arm, forearm and hand length, never one Arm Span slider. Upper arms trend toward greater absolute length and increased proportional contribution compared with human reference anatomy, but generally stay less visually dominant than the combined reach system.

> **Forearm length is a meaningful contributor to Grask racial anatomy and trends toward increased proportional contribution compared with Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy**, visible without becoming monstrous.

Forearm-to-upper-arm proportions vary under control (more balanced, or somewhat forearm-stronger), never reaching ape caricature, disconnected-looking arms or weapon-handling problems. Hands contribute to reach, but Grask reach never depends mainly on enormous hands; skeletal arm architecture is the major contributor.

## 28–37. Hands

> **Grask hands are large in absolute terms, proportionally long enough to integrate naturally with elongated forearms, and capable of broad palm/finger variation without becoming claws or oversized monster hands.**

Total hand length, palm length, breadth and depth, finger length and breadth, finger segment relationships and wrist breadth are separate, never one Hand Size concept. Palms stay substantial enough for grip, load transfer, tool and weapon use, never tiny narrow palms with long fingers. Approved tendency: **Grask trend toward somewhat greater finger length relative to palm dimensions than Marchfolk and Skarn reference anatomy**, reinforcing rangy distal anatomy while staying coherent and non-caricatured. Finger length, breadth, segment proportions and taper vary, with no spider-like fingers, claws or extremely narrow digits.

> **Grask possess five digits per hand and five digits per foot.** This is approved anatomy unless explicitly reopened.

Detailed nails come with later appearance work; claws aren't assumed, and ordinary protective nails compatible with fine manipulation are the baseline. **Grask hand proportions don't imply reduced fine motor control**; long fingers may change pose requirements but never intelligence or assumed skill. Grip validation covers relaxed hand, fist, precision pinch, small-object, one- and two-handed weapon, tool, ladder and climbing grips. Wrists give a credible transition from long forearm to large hand, never a narrow "break point."

## 38–44. Feet

> **Grask feet are large in absolute terms and proportioned to support tall, long-legged plantigrade anatomy, but they should not dominate the silhouette or become oversized fantasy troll feet.**

Absolute foot length, foot length relative to height, breadth, depth, heel, midfoot, forefoot, toe length and breadth and the ankle-foot transition are separate. Foot length suffices for support and locomotion but never scales linearly with height, with the proportional range subject to prototype validation. Breadth varies and may trend narrower in Narrow frames and broader in Broad ones, but stays relationship-aware rather than frame-locked. Toes stay humanoid and plantigrade, without clawed monster feet, long gripping toes or ape-like feet, and feet aren't prehensile. Foot size and tall stature affect base of support, which may matter for animation but never automatically creates balance penalties, stability bonuses or knockback effects (gameplay OPEN).

## 45–58. Frame and composition

Narrow, Balanced and Broad modify Grask skeletal breadth relationships only, never directly controlling height, limb, hand or foot length, muscularity or fat; statistical correlations may come later, but the concepts stay distinct. A **Narrow** Grask stays structurally credible, avoiding fragility caricature, pencil-thin joints, an extremely narrow chest and Aelari convergence. A **Broad** Grask stays rangy, avoiding Skarn convergence, Gorrund assumptions, a blocky torso and visually shortened limbs. Minimum, reference and maximum height each combine with narrow or broad frames, and none stops reading as Grask.

Physical composition stays separate from frame, with independent overall and regional muscularity and fat amount and distribution. Low-muscle Grask reveal skeletal relationships and never look starved, sickly, like tall humans or like Aelari with different ears. High muscle wraps Grask anatomy, following long upper arms, forearms, thighs, lower legs and Grask shoulders, never shortening the apparent limbs until the body reads Skarn-like. Regional muscle varies where valid in neck, shoulders, upper arms, forearms, chest, back, abdomen, glutes, thighs and lower legs, with the creator-facing control hierarchy provisional. Whether Grask muscular-development capacity differs from humans stays **OPEN**, and long visible muscles never imply greater strength or capacity. Fat accumulates over Grask anatomy, never turning it into generic large-humanoid anatomy; higher-fat Grask keep long limbs, reach, Grask shoulders and leg contribution, avoiding a generic obese human, ogre assumptions and a comedic troll belly. Fat distribution varies meaningfully without requiring belly, face or hip accumulation (population tendencies OPEN). High muscle with high fat is explicitly validated and stays Grask, not an accidental Gorrund prototype or oversized Skarn. Limb "thickness" is never undifferentiated: visible circumference comes from bone, muscle and fat, kept distinct.

## 59–64. Combined-proportion validity and extremes

Individually valid settings can combine into invalid anatomy, for example minimum torso contribution with maximum leg and neck length; maximum arm length, forearm emphasis and finger length; the narrowest frame with the smallest joints at maximum height; maximum torso depth with narrowest breadth and extreme composition; or maximum foot and toe length.

> **Grask validity must be relationship-aware, not slider-by-slider.**

No character combines every approved extreme, and a valid maximum in one dimension never guarantees compatibility with maxima elsewhere. There are no fixed subtypes, but future presets may explore valid tendencies (more torso-balanced, stronger leg or forearm emphasis, longer overall reach, narrow or broad structural frame) as phenotype variations, never subraces. GR-BODY-11 (longer-limbed extreme) stays coherent, never stilt-, spider- or ape-like or stretched by software; GR-BODY-10 (shorter-limbed extreme) keeps enough limb contribution to read Grask and fails as a tall human torso on ordinary human limbs. Cross-race biology comparisons neutralize composition where practical, so a muscular Skarn versus a low-muscle Grask is never read as a skeletal difference.

Chronological age, apparent biological age and age presentation stay separate, and the system supports visibly different younger, mature and older adults (descriptive categories, not brackets). Facial aging can involve fine lines, wrinkles, soft-tissue distribution, elasticity, eye, mouth and neck tissue, pigmentation and hair color and density, never a wrinkle overlay alone. Age never radically changes approved craniofacial identity; effects layer onto anatomy, and nose or ear enlargement is never exaggerated to look troll-like. Older Grask aren't automatically hunched, toothless, warty, bald, gaunt or frail (possible individual outcomes, not universal states), and younger adults keep full midface identity, ear architecture, limb proportions and craniofacial relationships. Age doesn't determine muscularity, fat or frame, so older muscular, higher-fat and lean Grask are all valid. Age-related thinning may exist with pattern and frequency **OPEN**, and baldness never automatically implies age.

## 49–58. Environment, acquired traits, presentation and lighting

> **Environmental appearance is not racial biology.**

Dirt, mud, dust, soot, sweat, rain or water, snow or frost, blood, ash and sand are environmental states, never phenotype. Default Grask are never muddy, wet, moss-covered, swamp-stained or slimy by troll convention; a muddy Grask needs an environmental reason. Every preset must be testable clean, dry, in neutral light, with no scars, tattoos, makeup or overlay, and still read Grask. Scars are acquired and vary in location, shape, size, age and tissue appearance, never assigned by race. Tattoos are personal presentation or cultural expression, with no mandatory "Grask tribal tattoos," and cosmetics and body paint are presentation (a green-painted non-green Grask stays biologically whatever their pigmentation is). Skin tones are validated clean before environmental effects so dirt never biases perceived pigmentation.

> **Biological appearance parameters ≠ observed lighting appearance.**

Olive, gray-green and umber may shift strongly under different light, and lighting artifacts are never encoded as pigmentation. Representative pigmentation is validated under neutral daylight-like, warm, cool and dim light (setup later), so it stays plausible without looking neon or dead.

# Part 4 clarification Pigmentation overlap

## 1–4. Human overlap and the surface-overlap principle

§3 never implied that every Grask tone differs from every human tone. Marchfolk's approved range already includes olive, tan, brown, deep brown and related warm and cool variations, so:

> **Individual Grask and humans may possess overlapping observed skin colors.**

Umber, ochre-brown, clay-brown, olive and olive-brown Grask may sit close to or inside similar human appearance ranges, which is valid. §3's population-level meaning is now:

> **The Grask pigmentation envelope as a whole extends into undertone and observed-color relationships outside the ordinary approved human envelope, particularly through some moss-olive, gray-olive and muted gray-green expressions. This does not require every Grask individual to possess a non-human-exclusive skin color.**

**Population distribution difference ≠ individual diagnostic trait.**

> **Skin pigmentation alone must never be required to classify an individual as Grask.**

A Grask whose skin overlaps Marchfolk stays clearly Grask through anatomy, and a human-colored surface on valid Grask anatomy never erases recognition. The broader project principle:

> **Different biological populations may possess overlapping surface phenotypes without becoming anatomically interchangeable.**

Overlap may occur in skin, hair and iris color, hair texture and other surface traits, and cross-population distinction is never manufactured by giving every race exclusive colors.

## 5–8. Vael overlap and no exclusive palettes

Vael pigmentation already includes cool, neutral and warm gray, ash-brown and related desaturated tones, and Grask may include gray-olive, muted gray-green, cool stone-gray-brown and related desaturated tones:

> **Some individual Vael and Grask may possess visually adjacent or partially overlapping surface coloration.**

This is acceptable, and neither range is changed to create separation. The Grask–Vael boundary test at about **198–203 cm** uses similar gray or desaturated pigmentation, comparable apparent age, neutral expression, controlled hair and presentation and controlled lighting, and the two stay distinct through skeletal architecture, torso relationships, limb contribution, joints, craniofacial anatomy and ear architecture, never skin color. The gray-skin test expands accordingly: a grayish Grask never reads undead, stone or a diseased human, and with coloration approaching Vael it still reads anatomically Grask; **if pigmentation is required to separate Grask from Vael, it fails.** Cross-race recognition is never solved with rigid palettes such as humans brown or tan, Vael gray, Grask green or Aelari pale; recognition survives realistic surface overlap.

# Part 5 Movement, animation, equipment, world interaction and final first-pass review

## 1–5. Core movement principle and posture

> **Grask movement must emerge from Grask anatomy rather than from a predefined "troll animation style."**

No lumbering, slow, clumsy, hunched, heavy-footed, aggressive, knuckle-dragging or bestial starting assumption is forced onto the anatomy. **Anatomical resting alignment** (the neutral state the anatomy supports) stays separate from **cultural or personal body language** (how an individual stands, gestures and moves), and personality or culture is never encoded in the locomotion baseline. The baseline is upright, bipedal, plantigrade, coherent with long limbs and compatible with ordinary manipulation, never requiring permanent spinal flexion, a forward monster hunch, a bent-knee crouch or ape-like arm hang. Reference- and maximum-height Grask stand naturally at full height, and world incompatibility is never solved by permanent slouching, hunching, bent knees or a lowered head; the world respects approved anatomy. The moderate-to-long neck supports neutral head carriage, looking up and down, dialogue eye contact, combat awareness and social interaction, and constant forward-head posture is never racial identity.

## 27–30. Reach architecture

**Anatomical arm reach** (what the body can reach), **interaction reach** (what gameplay lets the character activate), **combat reach** (how weapon and unarmed collision and attacks work) and **camera or targeting reach** (camera traces, targeting and selection) are separate and never automatically identical. Grask genuinely have greater anatomical reach tendencies through arm length relative to height, arm span, forearm and hand contribution, and this is never erased visually. Whether Grask get greater interaction, unarmed, pickup or use range is **OPEN**, and anatomical reach is never silently converted into gameplay advantage. Grask anatomy creates different unarmed reach geometry, but hitboxes, damage, attack range and speed and balance stay gameplay decisions, never inferred from size alone.

## 31–39. Weapons, shields, small objects and practicality

> **Weapon dimensions do not automatically scale with the wielder.**

A sword stays the same sword for Durrim, Marchfolk, Skarn, Aelari or Grask unless it's actually a different model or size; weapons are never enlarged for tall Grask or shrunk for shorter populations, and anatomy adapts to the object. One-handed swords, axes, maces, daggers where permitted and other tools are checked for grip size, wrist alignment, elbow path, body clearance and a weapon looking proportionally small, which never justifies enlarging it. Two-handed weapons are checked for hand spacing, shoulder range, elbow and torso clearance and weapon path, with long arms possibly needing different poses on the same weapon. Polearms are a critical test (long anatomy plus long weapon) for grips, hand separation, ready stance, attack arcs and world clearance, with no assumed superior gameplay reach. Bows are checked for long arms, Grask shoulders, draw posture, hand placement and face and ear clearance, never scaled, with impracticality **OPEN**. Shields are checked for grip, forearm, elbow, shoulder position and torso coverage, and a standard shield covering a different share of a Grask than a Durrim is physically expected. Large hands must still handle coins, keys, cups, books, food, handles and tools convincingly, never sacrificing fine manipulation. Some equipment may prove comfortable, awkward or impractical for some configurations; whether that creates restrictions is **OPEN**, never solved by scaling all equipment.
