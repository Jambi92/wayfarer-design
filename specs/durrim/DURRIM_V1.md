# Durrim Character Design v1.0 (first pass complete)

This is the Durrim specification, a genuinely distinct compact humanoid population. It's design only, with no UE5 changes. v1.0 arrived in five parts, all complete: Part 1 (biological foundation, stature and compact skeletal architecture), Part 2 (detailed body proportions, hands, feet and composition architecture), Part 3 (craniofacial anatomy, ears, hair and facial-hair biology, with three clarifications: facial identity, structural depth versus surface appearance, and facial depth validation criteria), Part 4 (pigmentation, skin, hair color, eyes, aging and environmental appearance) and Part 5 (movement expression, animation requirements, equipment fit and final validation). The final consistency review passed, so **Durrim Character Design v1.0 is FIRST-PASS COMPLETE.** That doesn't resolve every open biological question, finalize gameplay or technical architecture, or authorize UE5 implementation.

# Part 1 Biological foundation, stature and compact skeletal architecture

## 1–3. Core rule, identity and foundation

> **Durrim are not humans scaled down to dwarf height.**

Their short stature comes from their own coherent skeletal architecture, so a Durrim and a short Marchfolk at the same height still differ visibly in silhouette and anatomical relationships. Population tendencies: short overall stature; high skeletal structural presence relative to height; broad torso relationships; substantial thoracic depth; strong shoulder and pelvic integration; relatively substantial joints; shorter limb contribution to total height than the human reference; large hands and feet relative to stature; a low center of mass. These are skeletal tendencies and never require high muscularity or body fat, beards, a masculine look, heavy armor, mining, smithing, drinking or a gruff personality. Durrim are never made by uniformly scaling a Marchfolk down, shortening only the legs, enlarging only the torso or head, making a human broad and calling them a dwarf, or combining short height with maximum muscle. The whole anatomical system supports compact stature.

## 4–10. Height, proportions and neck

| Minimum | Reference | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 122 cm (4'0") | About 137 cm (4'6") | About 152 cm (5'0") |

This is the **provisional first-pass Durrim height range**, broad enough for individual variation while keeping population identity, with the statistical distribution left for later. **The overlap is intentional:** upper-range Durrim overlap lower Marchfolk and Sagekin, and a 152 cm Durrim and a 152 cm Marchfolk must never look interchangeable, differing in torso proportions, limb contribution, skeletal breadth, thoracic depth, joints, shoulders, pelvis and hand-foot proportions. This is a permanent validation case. Height is never uniform scale: shorter and taller Durrim differ through coordinated leg length, torso length, spinal and neck contribution, head-body relationships and limb segments, all inside Durrim validity.

**Compact doesn't mean compressed.** Durrim never look like a vertically squashed human skeleton: they look developed, functional, adult, weight-bearing and developmentally coherent, with no compressed joints or distorted bones used just to reduce height. **Adults read unmistakably as adults**, with no childlike head-body or facial proportions, hands, shoulders, pelvis, limbs or movement, so short stature never becomes visual infantilization. Durrim may have a somewhat greater head-to-total-height contribution than the Marchfolk Human Reference Population and other taller populations, but never an oversized fantasy-dwarf head: the head stays integrated with neck, shoulders, torso and overall mass, with craniofacial design later. The neck trends toward a relatively short contribution to total height, keeping absolute length, length relative to body, circumference, skeletal structure and muscular development separate, so Durrim can have substantial skeletal neck integration without large neck muscles.

## 11–17. Torso, shoulders, pelvis, spine and center of mass

The torso carries a major part of Durrim identity: a broad skeletal thorax relative to stature, substantial thoracic depth relative to stature, strong ribcage-spine integration, compact vertical torso relationships, and strong torso-pelvis continuity. It's never just "big": width, depth, vertical length, circumference, skeleton, muscle and fat stay distinct.

| Area | Tendency | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thoracic depth | Greater skeletal thoracic depth than equivalently tall Marchfolk, adding structural presence, front-to-back volume and compact mass distribution | Never automatically a large belly, high fat or huge chest muscles |
| Thoracic breadth | Greater skeletal ribcage breadth than equivalently tall Marchfolk | A Narrow Durrim stays recognizably Durrim, and a Broad one isn't a caricature |
| Shoulders | Substantial clavicles relative to stature, strong shoulder-joint presence, broad-shoulder potential, strong neck-shoulder transition | Shoulder breadth never comes from enlarged deltoids alone |
| Pelvis | A distinct architecture for compact stature, broad foundation, shorter lower limbs, low center of mass and stable locomotion | **OPEN, requires detailed anatomical design.** Never a simply widened human pelvis |
| Spine | Supports compact stature, deep and broad thorax, substantial upper-body mass potential, functional flexibility and stable locomotion | Never inherently stiff, since shorter doesn't mean less mobile |
| Center of mass | Lower than equivalently proportioned taller humanoids, from stature, compact torso, shorter limbs, pelvis and mass distribution | Never converted directly to gameplay bonuses yet |

## 18–26. Limbs, long bones, joints, hands and feet

Durrim have **lower average limb contribution to total height than equivalently tall Marchfolk**, especially the legs, but limbs are never stub-like and keep full adult articulation and believable segments. Arms trend shorter in absolute length and in contribution to height than Marchfolk, while possibly staying substantial relative to the compact torso, with upper arm, forearm, arm-to-torso ratio, joint scale and hand contribution distinguished, never uniformly shortened. Legs are a major part of the silhouette: shorter contribution to height than equivalently tall Marchfolk, functional long bones, adult knees and coherent hip-knee-ankle relationships, never made by shortening only the femur or lower leg.

Long bones have **greater skeletal structural presence relative to their length than equivalent Marchfolk anatomy**, so shorter never means thinner (substantial upper-arm, forearm, femoral and lower-leg structure), and this is skeleton, not muscle. Joints (shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles) trend toward substantial scale relative to limb length and stature, integrated with adjacent bones and never enlarged independently.

| Area | Tendency | Guard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hands | Relatively large and structurally substantial for total stature, with absolute and proportional length, palm breadth and depth, finger length and thickness, wrist structure and soft tissue kept separate | Not automatically muscular or calloused |
| Fingers | Varied finger length, breadth and palm-finger ratio within a Durrim structural envelope | Never universally short and thick, and crafting traditions never dictate hand anatomy |
| Feet | Relatively large and structurally substantial for height: broad skeletal base, strong ankle-foot integration, moderate relative length, strong weight-bearing architecture | Never comically oversized, and never derived from boots, mining, mountains, armor or barefoot traditions, since foot anatomy is biology and footwear is culture |

## 27–36. Structure versus muscle, frame, composition and mass

> **Durrim skeletal structural presence must never be represented through muscle alone** (mandatory).

A Durrim can have a broad ribcage, substantial joints, robust long bones and a broad pelvis with low muscularity, and a narrower Durrim can be extremely muscular. Skeletal frame is the continuous configuration, and Narrow, Balanced and Broad are frame presets within Durrim anatomy, not subtypes. A **Narrow Durrim** has less skeletal breadth, less broad shoulders and a narrower torso than other Durrim but keeps compact architecture and Durrim limb, joint, hand-foot relationships and identity. A **Broad Durrim** may strongly express shoulder, thoracic and pelvic breadth and structural presence, but Broad never automatically means muscular, fat, male or every skeletal dimension at maximum.

Durrim support full variation in muscularity, body-fat amount and distribution and regional development: lean, average, muscular, heavy, low-muscle high-fat, high-muscle high-fat and low-muscle low-fat within validity. **Durrim aren't universally stocky in composition:** the skeleton trends compact and substantial, the composition needn't. A low-fat Durrim clearly shows the compact skeleton, and a high-fat Durrim stays coherent rather than a generic round dwarf. Fat distribution isn't forced toward belly, waist or face, and sex-related differences depend on the unresolved universal system. Regional muscle (neck, shoulders, chest, back, arms, forearms, abdomen, glutes, thighs, calves) varies independently, with no automatic forearm, shoulder or back emphasis from labor stereotypes. Mass is never "human mass times dwarf scale": it comes from height, skeletal dimensions, structural density assumptions where appropriate, frame, muscle, fat and regional composition, with exact calculation left for later. The legacy roster's "0.65 human height, about 1.05 mass" is only an old signal that Durrim are unusually massive for their height, never literal. It's replaced by: **Durrim possess unusually high body volume and structural mass relative to their stature compared with Marchfolk, with exact mass determined by anatomy and composition.**

## 37–39. Sex-related anatomy, facial hair and culture

Durrim sex-related anatomy isn't finalized and stays separate from height, frame, muscle, fat, face, hair, culture and occupation, with no sex automatically broad, narrow, tall, short, muscular or bearded, pending the universal system (OPEN; the universal rule is now R-SEX in `decisions/PROJECT_RULES.md` — Durrim magnitudes stay OPEN). Facial-hair biology isn't finalized: not every Durrim grows a beard, facial hair isn't only male, beard density doesn't define adulthood, beard length isn't biological, and beardless Durrim aren't unusual. Facial-hair biology stays separate from beard grooming, presentation and culture, with detail in a later section. Mining, smithing, stoneworking, mountain living, clan loyalty, drinking, stubbornness, honor, greed, craftsmanship and warrior culture are never biological, though any may exist in Durrim cultures.

## 40–43. Movement, gameplay, legacy traits and the short-race rule

Movement will reflect anatomy (stature, low center of mass, limb proportions, joints, torso mass distribution, final composition), never automatically slow, clumsy, stomping, waddling, stiff or heavy-footed, with locomotion validated later. Compact anatomy never automatically grants strength, stability, knockback resistance, carry weight, reduced speed or jump, or more health, pending the Race to Biology to Gameplay Attributes Review. **Legacy traits:** "Durrim hold their breath longer" and "Durrim swim poorly" are **legacy or prototype gameplay traits subject to review**, and aren't yet justified by lung size, body density, limb length or culture.

> **The game's short playable races must not be three different scales of the same humanoid body** (locked).

Durrim stay anatomically distinct from future Pipkin and Cogling, which aren't pre-designed here. Durrim's defining short-race direction is **compact structural power and high skeletal presence relative to stature**, not a gameplay statement.

## 50–55. World, equipment, weapons, cameras, animation and technical foundation

The world must support approved Durrim anatomy: doors, stairs, ladders, chairs, beds, tables, counters, levers, shelves, windows, tunnels, conversation scenes, interactions, cameras, equipment, weapons, mounts, collision, navigation, animation and IK. Durrim bring significant vertical interaction differences, never solved by visually stretching the character during interactions. Equipment doesn't scale with Durrim height (a sword never becomes a miniature sword because a Durrim holds it), though race- or size-specific equipment may exist deliberately, and future architecture separates anatomy, equipment dimensions, fitting, grip placement, animation and collision or gameplay dimensions. Weapons are never fitted by uniform scaling, and grip spacing, clearance, two-handed weapons, reach, ground intersection, sheathing, draw animations and camera framing need testing, with combat balance OPEN. Cameras account for Durrim stature rather than Marchfolk camera height, without assuming camera equals eye position in every mode (first- and third-person architecture OPEN). The shared human animation is a prototype placeholder, and Durrim may need retargeting, stride adjustment, foot and hand placement, IK, interaction adaptation, dedicated locomotion or procedural adjustment, with no architecture chosen. The technical foundation is OPEN (modified shared humanoid hierarchy, dedicated Durrim skeleton, custom mesh, procedural bone proportions, morph and deformation, retargeting, IK, hybrid), MetaHuman suitability isn't assumed, and design requirements drive the evaluation.

# Part 2 Detailed body proportions, hands, feet and composition architecture

## 1–3. Core rule, direction and dimensions

> **No individual body part should look shortened, widened or enlarged in isolation.**

The body works as one coherent structure. Compared with equivalently tall Marchfolk, Durrim trend toward greater torso and lower leg and overall limb contribution to total height, greater skeletal torso breadth and thoracic depth, greater skeletal presence relative to stature, larger hands and feet relative to height, and more substantial joints, all as tendencies with overlap. Every parameter distinguishes, where relevant, **absolute dimension**, **dimension relative to total height** and **dimension relative to adjacent anatomy**. For example, hands can be large relative to stature without being absolutely larger than a much taller Skarn's. Terms like "big hands," "thick legs" or "huge torso" aren't used without naming the relationship.

## 4–14. Torso, ribcage, abdomen, shoulders, neck and pelvis

Durrim have a greater torso contribution to height than equivalently tall Marchfolk, which doesn't mean an absolutely long torso: the legs contribute less while the compact torso keeps substantial vertical, transverse and depth structure. The torso stays vertically compact, never an extremely long torso on short legs, a vertically compressed human torso or a near-square block, with recognizable thorax, abdomen, waist and pelvis transitions.

| Area | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Ribcage | Skeletal breadth, depth, vertical length, lower-ribcage relationships and spine integration vary independently. Averages trend broader and deeper relative to stature than equivalent-height Marchfolk |
| Ribcage versus shoulders | Not locked together: broad ribcage with moderate shoulders, moderate ribcage with broad shoulders, or a narrower Durrim ribcage with moderate shoulders are all valid, with coherent clavicles and shoulder joints |
| Thoracic depth versus fat | Skeletal depth isn't belly size, chest muscle or body fat. A lean Durrim can have substantial front-to-back skeletal depth, a major validation case |
| Abdomen and waist | No required large belly, fat-thick waist, straight waist or barrel silhouette. Waist skeleton, abdominal soft tissue, muscle and fat amount and distribution vary, and a narrow waist is valid with coherent ribcage and pelvis |
| Shoulder breadth | Skeletal, from clavicle length, shoulder-joint position, ribcage breadth and upper-thoracic structure, never muscle alone |
| Shoulder presence | Joints more structurally substantial relative to stature than equivalent-height Marchfolk, never huge deltoids, permanently raised shoulders, no visible neck or a hunched posture |
| Neck | Relatively short proportional contribution, but always with a visible transition into the shoulder girdle, never the head attached straight to the torso |
| Pelvis | Exact morphology stays OPEN. Only strong torso-pelvis integration, a compact-stature pelvis, stable hip placement, coherent support for shorter legs and broad variation are set |
| Pelvic breadth | Frame preset may shift skeletal pelvic breadth, which stays distinct from hip fat, glutes and thigh muscle, and external hip width never stands in for skeletal width |

## 15–25. Arms and hands

Arms trend shorter in absolute length and lower in proportional contribution than Marchfolk, but stay long enough for natural self-contact, tool use, weapon handling, climbing and everyday interaction, never exaggeratedly short for silhouette. The upper arm varies in absolute length, length relative to torso, skeletal diameter and joints, trending toward substantial skeletal structure relative to its length, separate from biceps and triceps. The forearm varies independently in length, skeletal structure and wrist transition, with no crafting-stereotype huge forearms, since a substantial forearm skeleton can sit under low muscle. Upper-arm and forearm lengths vary independently within relationship-aware limits, with no fixed arm ratio. Elbows follow the upper-arm, forearm and joint relationship, never enlarged independently or used as dwarf shorthand. Wrists trend toward substantial presence relative to hand and forearm length, coupled to forearm, hand base and frame.

Hands are an important population identifier, trending toward a greater hand size relative to stature than Marchfolk, substantial palm structure and strong wrist-hand integration, while staying highly customizable. Absolute length, length relative to stature and to forearm, palm length, breadth and depth, finger length, breadth and segment proportions, and wrist breadth are independent but relationship-aware, never one Hand Size slider. A greater palm share of total hand length is a **provisional population tendency**, but fingers aren't universally short and relatively long fingers are valid. Finger structure allows fine manipulation: substantial hands never reduce dexterity, precision or fine motor control, and never grant superior crafting, since skill is separate from anatomy. Skeletal hand structure stays separate from muscle, fat, calluses, scars and wear: a scholar and a stoneworker can share similar hand skeletons with very different hands, and occupation never determines inherited anatomy.

## 26–35. Legs, feet and limb structure

Legs are proportionally shorter relative to stature than equivalent-height Marchfolk, through coordinated pelvis, femur, knee, lower leg, ankle and foot relationships, never one bone alone. The femur trends toward reduced absolute length and contribution to height with substantial structure relative to length, never cartoonishly short, with functional hips and knees. The lower leg trends the same way, and femur and lower leg needn't shorten by identical percentages, since segment variation is desirable. Knee scale responds to femoral and lower-leg structure, frame and weight-bearing, never large knees as a cosmetic dwarf feature. Ankles integrate strongly with lower leg and foot while keeping broad mobility, never fusing lower leg and foot into a block.

Feet support a stable compact skeleton: relatively large dimensions for stature, substantial skeletal breadth, strong ankle-foot transition and a stable base. Absolute length, length relative to stature, breadth, skeletal depth, heel, forefoot breadth, toe length and proportions and the ankle relationship are distinct, never one Foot Size control, and feet are never uniformly enlarged: moderate relative length with high breadth, greater relative length with moderate breadth, or a compact foot with strong skeletal depth are all valid. The foot, pelvis and leg architecture gives a broad potential base of support that may affect movement appearance, never automatically gameplay stability, knockback resistance or balance bonuses. "Thick limbs" isn't used without specifying tissue (skeletal diameter, muscle, fat, total circumference), and Durrim trend toward greater skeletal presence relative to limb length than equivalent-height Marchfolk.

## 36–40. Frame, height and composition independence

| Frame preset | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Narrow | May reduce ribcage, shoulder, pelvic and long-bone breadth and joint scale relative to other Durrim, while staying inside Durrim-specific minimum structural relationships and never collapsing into short Marchfolk anatomy |
| Balanced | A central creator starting point, never the canonical, most correct or default NPC Durrim body |
| Broad | May increase skeletal ribcage, shoulder and pelvic breadth and joint presence within coherent limits, never every dimension equally |

Frame doesn't determine height: Narrow and Broad Durrim can each be short, average or tall, with no fixed height-frame packages. Frame doesn't determine composition either: Narrow isn't lean and Broad isn't muscular or fat, and Narrow muscular, Narrow heavy, Broad lean, Broad low-muscle, Balanced very muscular and Balanced high-fat are all valid.

## 41–49. Muscle and fat

Muscularity operates over Durrim anatomy rather than replacing it, with regional development across neck, trapezius, shoulders, chest, back, upper arms, forearms, abdomen, glutes, thighs and calves. **Low-muscle Durrim (permanent validation)** keep substantial joints, compact torso, Durrim limb proportions and hand-foot relationships, and it fails if removing muscle makes them look human. High-muscle Durrim stay believable, with muscle developing around the skeleton rather than inflated arms, shoulders and chest, avoiding bodybuilder caricature unless deliberately chosen within extreme valid composition. Body-fat amount has a broad range, with no artificially high minimum to keep the silhouette, since the skeleton carries racial identity. Fat distribution (face and neck, upper torso, abdomen, waist, hips, glutes, upper arms, thighs) is separate from amount, with sex-related patterns OPEN. A very lean Durrim shows ribcage, shoulders, pelvis, joints and limb structure without looking unhealthy by default, since lean isn't frail. A heavy Durrim stays structurally Durrim, never converging on a spherical torso, huge belly, tiny legs or the comedic dwarf silhouette. Durrim with substantial skeleton, muscle and fat together may be visually massive for their height while keeping anatomical transitions, not one block. Advanced regional variation (stronger upper or lower body, larger thighs or calves, more developed back or arms) is never tied to occupation.

## 50–53. Combined validity, presets and limb variation

Individually valid controls can become invalid together, so validation is relationship-aware, for example maximum torso breadth with minimum pelvis and shoulders, minimum wrist with maximum palm breadth, minimum ankle with maximum foot breadth, a very short femur with maximum lower-leg length, or minimum joint scale with maximum long-bone breadth. Sliders are never validated independently. Possible starting presets (compact or central, narrow structural, broad structural, longer-limbed, shorter-limbed, lean, muscular, heavy) are appearance starting points, never castes or subraces. Longer-limbed Durrim stay inside the Durrim envelope, never becoming "short Marchfolk with a Durrim head," and shorter-limbed Durrim stay functional adults, never childlike, caricatured, with implausible gait or compressed joints.

# Part 3 Craniofacial anatomy, ears, hair and facial-hair biology

## 1–2. Core facial rule and adult identity

> **A Durrim must still look Durrim when bald, clean-shaven, with neutral expression and with the ears hidden.**

Identity never depends on a beard, large nose, heavy eyebrows, wrinkles, helmet, hairstyle or cultural presentation. Faces read as fully adult across the whole stature range, never with childlike cranial proportions, oversized childlike eyes, a small juvenile jaw, a tiny nose, an infant-like midface or a cranium excessively large for the facial skeleton.

## 3–7. Cranial and facial foundation

Provisional tendencies for a distinct compact craniofacial foundation: strong cranial-neck integration, substantial facial skeletal presence relative to body stature, compact vertical facial relationships, strong midface integration, substantial jaw-joint relationships and broad facial variation. That never means every Durrim has a wide or square face, heavy brow or large jaw. Cranial height isn't exaggerated because Durrim are short: the head may contribute somewhat more to stature than in the Marchfolk Human Reference Population and other taller populations, but the skull stays adult and coherent, keeping absolute cranial height, cranial height relative to face and head height relative to body separate. **Greater cranial breadth relative to cranial height than the Marchfolk reference distribution is a provisional population tendency**, and narrower Durrim crania stay valid. Facial width varies independently through zygomatic, temporal, mandibular and midface breadth, never one Face Width scalar. Durrim trend toward relatively compact vertical facial relationships compared with Marchfolk (lower vertical contribution relative to breadth and depth), never a vertically squashed human face, with forehead, orbits, midface, mouth and jaw coherent.

## 29–34. External ears

Durrim ears are broadly humanoid in organization, with a coherent auricular root, helix, antihelix, concha, tragus, antitragus and lobe, never simple attached shapes. Durrim don't have pointed ears and stay biologically distinct from the shared elven ear architecture, avoiding accidental elf-like taper. Absolute length, length relative to head, breadth, projection, orientation, curvature, lobe size and attachment, position and asymmetry vary, never one Ear Size control. There's no strong universal ear-size stereotype yet: first-pass Durrim ears sit within a broad compact-humanoid range, with population distributions future work. Projection runs from close-set to more projecting, independent of length, breadth and lobe size, and projecting ears are never comic short-stature shorthand. Biological ear anatomy stays separate from acquired cuts, missing sections, scars and injury deformation, and occupational damage is never racial.

## 35–39. Hair biology

**Hair biology** (natural pigmentation, texture, density, hairline, growth distribution, age change) stays separate from **hair presentation** (length, style, braids, shaving, ornamentation, grooming). Texture isn't restricted: straight, wavy, curly, and tightly curled or coiled where appropriate to final population design, with frequencies OPEN. Scalp-hair density varies, never universally thick-haired, bald-resistant or hairy, with age thinning and individual variation possible. Hairline height, temple shape, widow's peak where appropriate, age recession and asymmetry vary, and hairline isn't locked to forehead anatomy. Pigmentation families and frequencies come in Part 4, with no assumption of universally dark, red, brown or gray hair, and natural silver or white hair, if later allowed before aging, stays distinct from age depigmentation.

## 40–49. Facial hair, eyebrows, body hair and hair-equipment interaction

Facial-hair biology (follicle distribution, density, texture, coverage, growth rate where relevant, age change) is designed separately from beard culture, with Durrim distributions provisional until sex-related anatomy is resolved. Beard length, shape, braiding, ornamentation, shaving, trimming and styling are **personal presentation**, not skeletal biology.

> **A clean-shaven Durrim must look completely biologically valid and unmistakably adult** (locked). Beards are never needed to make the face work.

# Part 3 clarification 2: structural depth versus surface appearance

> **Craniofacial structural depth describes the three-dimensional relationships of the underlying craniofacial skeleton. Surface appearance describes the visible soft tissue, skin and presentation layered over that structure** (locked).

Structural depth is the front-to-back relationship among frontal bone and brow, orbital rims, cranial base, zygomatic structure, midface, maxilla, nasal skeletal root, mandible and jaw joint. It's an anatomical geometry concept, not a texture or visual-effect parameter, and it's multiregional (brow and orbit, cheek and zygomatic, central midface, maxillary, mandibular), coupled but not identical, never one Face Depth scalar.

| Kept distinct from structural depth | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Feature projection | How far one feature projects relative to its surroundings. Substantial depth needn't mean a projecting nose, chin or heavy brow, and one projecting feature doesn't make the face deep |
| Facial width | Independent dimensions: narrower and deeper, broader and shallower, broader and deeper, or narrower with moderate depth are all valid, so broad never automatically means deep |
| Facial verticality | Compact verticality doesn't create depth, and depth doesn't require a shorter face, though they may correlate probabilistically |
| Surface brow | Skeletal brow geometry is separate from eyebrow hair, skin, fat, muscle, age, expression and lighting, so strong skeletal brows with subtle eyebrows, or thick eyebrows over subtle bone, are both possible |
| Deep-set eye look | Orbital structural depth is separate from the deep-set impression created by orbit, lids, brow tissue, eye position, facial fat and shadow |
| Nasal projection | Its own dimension, never a depth substitute: substantial midface depth with modest nose, or moderate depth with strong nose, are both valid Durrim |
| Mandibular width and chin | Mandibular depth (body, ramus, jaw-joint position, chin relationship, lower-face front-to-back structure) is separate from jaw width, chin projection, masseter size, facial fat and beard volume |
| Muscle, fat, skin | Muscle and fat change surface contours, never the skeleton, so lean and high-fat Durrim can share identical structural depth. Skin thickness, texture, wrinkles, pores, scarring and weathering are surface appearance |
| Age and expression | Aging changes soft tissue, elasticity, fat, muscle, contours and appropriate skeletal features, but isn't simulated by changing racial depth parameters. Expression never modifies skeletal identity, so a neutral-expression validation view is mandatory |
| Lighting and materials | **Observed facial depth under a particular light is not the biological structural-depth value**, so anatomy is validated under neutral reference light first. Racial structure is never faked mainly through normal maps, displacement, roughness, ambient occlusion, baked shadow or skin materials: large-scale depth lives in geometry or deformation, fine detail may use materials later, and implementation is OPEN |

**Facial Diagnostic Domains** (renamed by the consistency-resolution patch, for facial analysis only, never Character Architecture Layers and never called "Layer A," "Layer B" and so on): **FD-STRUCT** structural anatomy (cranium and facial skeleton); **FD-SOFT** biological soft tissue (muscle, adipose and other soft-tissue volume); **FD-SURF** skin surface (pigmentation, texture, age surface effects, local variation, scars where appropriate); **FD-HAIR** hair (eyebrows, facial and scalp hair); **FD-PRES** personal presentation (cosmetics, tattoos, grooming, jewelry); **FD-OBS** observational conditions (lighting, camera, expression, pose). Structural depth is evaluated with neutral expression, neutral reference lighting, minimal facial hair, neutral skin material where useful, and matched age and composition, comparing actual geometry so surface appearance never masquerades as racial anatomy. DU-DEPTH-01 to 03 (lower, central and greater valid depth, matched in width, verticality, nose, jaw breadth, composition, age, hair and expression) test whether depth works as its own dimension. The surface-variation test puts one identical skeleton under leaner or fuller soft tissue, different eyebrows, facial hair, older surface, different skin texture and lighting, and the structural identity should stay recognizable. Cross-race comparisons against Marchfolk, Sagekin, Skarn, Pipkin and Cogling use matched height where useful, age, composition, expression and lighting, and each names the population and region, never "more than everyone else."

> **Durrim trend toward greater average craniofacial skeletal depth relative to facial vertical height than equivalent Marchfolk anatomy, expressed through multiregional craniofacial relationships rather than through nasal projection or any single feature** (provisional population tendency until comparative validation).

**Universal principle (promoted):** **structural anatomy, biological soft tissue, surface appearance, presentation, expression and observed lighting appearance are separate concepts**, for every race. The universal facial-control hierarchy stays deferred.

# Part 3 clarification 3: facial depth validation criteria (resolved)

> **Craniofacial structural depth must be validated through multiple anatomical relationships, not through one subjective impression or one global Face Depth measurement** (locked).

There's no approved Durrim facial depth number yet, and total head depth alone isn't enough, since vault shape, nose or chin projection or occipital shape can inflate it without changing the relationships being described. Validation uses a consistent anatomical reference system (vertical, left-right and front-back axes) with stable landmarks, never hair, beard, wrinkles, expression or camera perspective, with landmark implementation later. The neutral validation state is neutral expression and head orientation, a standardized camera and focal length, neutral reference lighting, no beard over landmarks, hair removed or controlled, matched apparent age and facial soft tissue where practical, with final evaluation on actual geometry.

| Depth domain | Evaluate | Exclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A. Brow and supraorbital | Frontal bone, supraorbital region, orbital rim, upper nasal root | Eyebrow hair, expression, shadow intensity |
| B. Orbital | Orbital rim, eye position, surrounding cranial structure | Eyeball size and position, eyelids, visible opening (a deep-set look doesn't prove orbital depth) |
| C. Zygomatic and cheek | Zygomatic projection and its relationship to orbit and midface, front-to-back cheek skeleton | Cheek fat, and lateral cheekbone width confused with forward projection |
| D. Midface and maxillary (especially important for Durrim) | Front-to-back relationships among orbit, zygoma, maxilla, nasal root and base, upper alveolar region | Nose-tip projection |
| E. Mandibular and lower face | Mandibular body, ramus, jaw-joint position, lower alveolar structure, chin relationship | Jaw width, chin projection, masseter volume (a projecting chin isn't mandibular depth) |

The five domains are related, never five fully independent sliders. **Exclusion rules:** nasal-tip projection isn't the primary depth landmark (nasal skeleton still counts, but nose projection is analyzed separately); the most anterior chin point alone isn't lower-face depth; depth is never inferred from face, zygomatic, jaw or cranial width; and depth is never inferred from compact facial height alone. **Absolute structural depth** (actual front-to-back distance between landmarks) and **proportional structural depth** (depth relative to facial height, cranial or regional dimensions) are both recorded. The Durrim tendency is mainly proportional, so not every absolute Durrim depth exceeds Marchfolk.

**Comparison methods:** raw 137 cm Durrim against 173 cm Marchfolk measurements are never compared directly, since that confounds anatomy with body size. Instead: an equal-height comparison (permanent), about 150 cm Durrim against about 150 cm Marchfolk where ranges overlap, matched in age, composition, sex-related state once defined, expression and presentation; a population-reference comparison of reference Durrim and reference Marchfolk read in both absolute and proportional terms; and a diagnostic-only same-facial-height comparison to see whether depth differences survive when vertical compactness is controlled, never resizing actual characters.

A strong-depth Durrim shows increased depth coherently across several related regions, not one projecting region, though not equally everywhere. It fails on incoherent combinations such as extreme brow projection with incompatible orbits, a deep maxilla disconnected from the zygoma, large mandibular depth on an implausibly small jaw joint, or strong cheek projection with incompatible orbit and midface. **DU-DEPTH-01** (lower valid depth) must still read as Durrim, so the race never depends on maximum depth. **DU-DEPTH-02** (central) is a validation configuration, not the canonical face. **DU-DEPTH-03** (greater valid depth) comes from coherent multiregional skeleton, never a bigger nose or chin, brow alone, stronger normal maps or added shadow.

| Test | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Matched nose | With nasal anatomy matched across DU-DEPTH-01 to 03, depth differences stay visible and measurable, or the system is using nose projection as a substitute |
| Matched brow | With brow structure matched and other domains varied, heavy brow isn't the main depth mechanism |
| Matched jaw | With jaw breadth and chin projection matched and mandibular and midface depth varied, depth isn't just jaw size |
| Soft-tissue neutralization | The same skeleton with several facial soft-tissue states keeps the same skeletal depth classification, and fails if added facial fat changes it |
| Lighting neutralization | Under neutral frontal, side, overhead and diffuse light, perceived strength may change but biological classification must not |
| Perspective | A standardized diagnostic camera, since focal length distorts nose, midface, width and chin, never judging from close wide-angle shots (focal length chosen during technical validation) |
| Silhouette and profile | Front, three-quarter and profile together, since profile shows projection and the other views show integration |
| Cross-section | Internal landmark or cross-section visualization as a validation tool if tools allow, never a player-facing requirement |
| Population validation | Substantial Durrim and Marchfolk samples compared as overlapping distributions: both broad, with the Durrim center shifted toward greater proportional depth, never "every Durrim above every Marchfolk" or a hard "below this value is human" boundary |

**Recognition versus measurement:** anatomical validation (are the relationships actually different) and visual recognition (does the whole character read as Durrim) are both required, since a character can pass measurement but fail visually, and a stereotypical beard and nose can signal "dwarf" while failing the anatomy. **No numerical thresholds yet** (no millimeters, percentages, ratios or minimum depth scores): v1.0 locks the measurement concepts and methodology. **Prototype calibration later:** once Durrim and Marchfolk prototype heads exist, establish landmarks, build representative samples, measure regional relationships, compare distributions, run blinded visual comparisons where useful, identify combinations that read correctly, adjust validity envelopes, and only then consider numerical limits, never inventing numbers first. **Future requirement:** the eventual facial architecture must represent these regional depth relationships without one global slider, which isn't permission to design it now.

> **Resolved Durrim criterion:** Durrim provisionally trend toward greater average craniofacial skeletal depth relative to facial vertical height than equivalent Marchfolk anatomy. This tendency must be expressed and validated through coherent multiregional brow/orbital, zygomatic, midface/maxillary and mandibular relationships, rather than nasal projection, chin projection, facial width, soft tissue, surface materials, lighting or expression.

This replaces vague statements like "Durrim have deeper faces." **Durrim v1.0 Part 3 facial depth validation criteria are resolved**, without numerical ranges, which depend on prototype validation. Part 3 stays complete, Durrim v1.0 stays in progress, and the Universal Facial Customization Architecture stays deferred until all 13 first-pass races are complete.

# Part 4 Pigmentation, skin, hair color, eyes, aging and environmental appearance

## 1–2. Core principle and appearance architecture

Durrim pigmentation and surface appearance represent a biologically plausible population with broad individual variation, never fantasy-dwarf stereotypes: universally pale or ruddy skin, red noses, soot-covered faces, mining grime, universally dark, red or thick hair, gray beards by default, or weathered skin as biology. Biology, environment, age and presentation stay separate. Appearance analysis distinguishes biological pigmentation, biological skin properties, biological soft tissue, hair biology, age-related biological change, acquired environmental effects, personal presentation and observational lighting, using the Facial Diagnostic Domains (FD-STRUCT, FD-SOFT, FD-SURF, FD-HAIR, FD-PRES, FD-OBS) where applicable, never called Character Architecture Layers.

## 3–13. Skin

| Topic | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Pigmentation principle and range | A broad natural envelope, independent of the compact skeleton: lighter, intermediate and darker complexions, with warm, cool and neutral undertones. Frequencies aren't finalized, and Marchfolk distributions aren't simply copied without comparative review |
| Dimensions | Never one Skin Color slider: melanin-related pigmentation, undertone, local vascular contribution, local pigment variation, age-related and environmentally acquired change, with the model future work |
| Base versus surface | Base pigmentation stays distinct from sun exposure, flushing, dirt, soot, dust, bruising, scarring, weathering, cosmetics and lighting, none of which redefines biology |
| Ruddy appearance | Never biologically required. Ruddiness may come from vascular visibility, temperature, exertion, environment, age, skin characteristics or lighting, and red cheeks and nose are never a Durrim marker |
| Regional variation | Plausible non-uniform variation across face, ears, neck, hands, arms, torso, knees, elbows and feet (patterns future work), never painted-on gradients |

## 14–20. Hair color

There's no canonical Durrim hair color. The first-pass natural envelope may include black, very dark brown, brown, lighter brown, blond and darker blond where valid, auburn, red and copper where valid, and naturally low-pigment variants if later justified, with frequencies OPEN and red hair never assumed disproportionately common. Natural hair allows subtle variation (roots, strands, sun lightening, age depigmentation, facial-hair differences), not implemented yet. Facial hair is usually biologically related to scalp hair but needn't match (slightly lighter or darker, warmer or cooler, different depigmentation timing), and eyebrows likewise. No system assigns one identical color value to scalp hair, eyebrows, facial hair and body hair. Hair dye is **personal presentation** (natural-looking, artificial, partial, cultural patterns), with no Durrim dye practices established. Early graying is individual variation, never a Durrim stereotype.

## 21–28. Eyes

Iris anatomy, iris pigmentation, pupil, sclera, cornea, lighting and reflection, and magical effects stay separate. First-pass iris families may include brown, dark brown, hazel, amber, green, gray and blue, with distributions OPEN and human frequencies not automatically copied. Natural iris color is never glow, emissive, magical, supernatural patterning, class or status effects, so a naturally amber-eyed Durrim isn't inherently magical. Irises have internal variation (radial, central and peripheral differences, crypts and fibers, pigment density), never flat disks, with implementation OPEN. Natural heterochromia (complete, sectoral, central) may be supported where appropriate, with frequency OPEN and never inflated for distinctiveness. Sclera isn't unnaturally bright white by default, responding to biology, age, vascularity, later health-state systems and lighting, and disease is never encoded through racial appearance. Pupils stay broadly humanoid, with no slit or unusual geometry or glow added for fantasy. **OPEN:** Durrim specialized visual adaptation. Low-light vision isn't granted because fantasy dwarves live underground, and any later decision needs biological, worldbuilding and gameplay justification.

## 29–39. Aging

Durrim visibly age, never ageless and never all middle-aged or elderly. Lifespan, maturation, fertility, senescence and age-stage durations are **OPEN**, never derived from fantasy-dwarf conventions. Chronological age, apparent biological age and age presentation stay distinct, and apparent age isn't inferred from chronological age until lifecycle biology exists. Creation eventually supports younger, mature, older and elderly adults, with chronological mapping later. Aging is never just "wrinkles plus gray hair": it may affect facial soft tissue, skin, hair, posture, possibly skeletal appearance and body composition.

| Aspect | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Face | Fine lines, deeper folds, facial fat changes, eye and mouth changes, jaw and neck surface changes, skin texture, always respecting Durrim craniofacial architecture, so age never replaces racial anatomy |
| Structural depth | Age-related surface changes are never mistaken for more craniofacial structural depth: an older Durrim may look more angular from soft tissue without their depth classification rising, under the approved validation rules |
| Hair | Reduced pigment, mixed gray, white, density, hairline and texture changes, individually varied, never complete whitening at a fixed age |
| Beard | Where present, may age differently from scalp hair (earlier or later, patchy or mixed), and beard color is never a chronological-age meter |
| Skin | Fine lines, wrinkles, elasticity, pigment variation, texture and exposure changes, keeping biological aging and environmental exposure distinguishable where possible |
| Body | Muscle volume, fat distribution, skin, posture, movement and hair, never assuming every older Durrim becomes frail |

## 40–47. Environmental appearance and presentation

**Biological appearance** stays strongly separate from **environmental appearance** (dirt, mud, dust, soot, sweat, water, blood, snow and frost, rain wetness, sun exposure, weathering), which are transient environmental states (Environmental Skin Appearance Layer).

> **Soot, stone dust and mining grime are not Durrim racial features** (permanent rule).

If such systems exist, they come from location, occupation, activity and environment, so a Marchfolk miner and a Durrim miner in the same conditions both use the same environmental system. Weathering may reflect sun, wind, cold, dryness, occupation, age and lifestyle, and Durrim aren't universally weathered. Scars are acquired unless a specific biological mechanism says otherwise, varying in location, size, shape, age, severity and healing, and never disproportionately common in presets to signal toughness. Burns and injuries are acquired, not racial, with injury customization in later universal design. Tattoos are personal presentation and cultural expression, with no Durrim tradition established and no rune tattoos invented because they're dwarves. Cosmetics are presentation, available by cultural and personal systems and never sex-locked by default. Cosmetics, paint, tattoos, dirt overlays and ritual markings layer over biological pigmentation rather than overwriting it.

## 48–50. Lighting

> **Biological appearance parameters are not the same as observed appearance under lighting.**

Skin, hair and iris may look different under sunlight, firelight, moonlight, interior or magical light, and biological values are never changed to compensate for one environment. Validation uses standardized neutral reference lighting for skin, undertone, hair, iris and age surface effects, then world lighting, with extreme tests in bright and overcast daylight, warm firelight, cool low light, deep shadow and mixed light. Materials stay believable without losing identity, and Durrim skin or eyes are never artificially brightened to stay visible in darkness.

## 62–66. Randomization, presets, technical architecture, decisions and status

Biological randomization will consider population distributions, valid relationships, age, pigmentation, hair biology and iris, with presentation randomization separate and nothing implemented yet. Presets show broad biological diversity and never lean disproportionately on beard, red or gray hair, pale or ruddy skin, wrinkles, soot or mining clothing, as legitimate outputs of the same system. Technical architecture is OPEN (skin materials, pigmentation parameters, subsurface scattering, hair shading, eye materials, age morphs, wrinkle systems, environmental overlays, dirt, wetness and blood, scars, tattoos and cosmetics).

First-pass approved: broad natural skin pigmentation, with frequencies OPEN; skin pigmentation independent of the skeleton; ruddiness not required; skin toughness or thickness differences OPEN; environmental adaptation OPEN; broad natural hair pigmentation, with red hair neither required nor privileged; scalp hair, eyebrows and facial hair related but not identical; broad natural iris pigmentation, and iris color isn't a racial identifier; specialized low-light vision OPEN; Durrim visibly age, with lifecycle OPEN; aging affects more than wrinkles and hair color and preserves racial anatomy; environmental appearance is separate from biology; soot and mining grime aren't racial; scars are acquired; tattoos and cosmetics are presentation; biology stays separate from observed lighting; no automatic pigmentation-phenotype packages; appearance technical architecture OPEN. **Durrim v1.0 Part 4 is complete**, and v1.0 as a whole isn't.

# Part 5 Movement expression, animation requirements, equipment fit and final validation

## 1–5. Core movement principle, posture and center of mass

> **Durrim movement emerges from Durrim anatomy.**

Durrim are never animated as scaled-down, slow, heavy or comedic humans, or as constantly stomping or waddling characters. Movement reflects short stature, compact skeletal architecture, lower limb contribution, substantial joints, relatively large feet, a lower center of mass, broad or deep torso possibilities, and the full range of frame and composition. **Anatomical movement constraints** (stride geometry, reach, joint relationships, center-of-mass behavior, contact points) stay separate from **cultural or personal body language**: biology never requires swagger, stomping, aggression, gruff or proud posture, hands-on-belt posing or drunken movement. The neutral resting alignment is an ordinary relaxed stance, never a permanent crouch, hunch, bowed legs, wide combat stance or forward lean, and individual variation in spinal posture, shoulder and head carriage, pelvic orientation, foot orientation and stance width never erases Durrim skeletal identity. A generally lower center of mass is an emergent biomechanical consequence of approved anatomy, not automatically knockback resistance, stability, a combat advantage or a movement penalty; any gameplay consequence is OPEN.

## 6–18. Locomotion

| Topic | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Walking | Accounts for shorter absolute legs, the leg-to-torso relationship, Durrim pelvis, knee and ankle structure, feet, height, frame and composition; never a sped-up or slowed-down human walk |
| Stride | Shorter absolute stride potential than much taller humanoids, but stride also depends on limb proportions, joint motion, gait, speed and individual style; never "short race = tiny steps" |
| Cadence | Evaluated biomechanically at a given world speed; never comically rapid or slow-motion heavy steps |
| Movement speed | **Stature alone doesn't determine gameplay movement speed.** Animation must support whatever movement model is approved without severe foot sliding or distortion |
| Running and sprinting | Reflects compact architecture, legs, feet, center of mass and composition; neither naturally slow nor naturally a sprinter (performance OPEN). Sprint stays viable across minimum, reference and maximum height, all frames, low and high muscle and higher body fat, with no body configuration needing a different kind of locomotion |
| Turning | Slow, fast and running turns, pivots and reversals, avoiding exaggerated "tank-like" turning |
| Starting and stopping | Accounts for mass, center of mass, leg length and foot contact, without implying every Durrim is enormously heavy because of high structural presence |
| Crouching and kneeling | Accounts for torso depth, pelvis, femur, knee, ankle mobility and composition, with no torso-thigh intersection or forced wide knees; one-knee, two-knee and rising validated, later with armor |
| Jumping and landing | Anatomically valid, with jump height, distance and athletic capability never inferred from body shape (gameplay decisions); landings account for foot, ankle, knee, hip, pelvis, spine and composition, with no ground-shaking "dwarf landings" unless a real gameplay event causes them |
| Falling | Fall and recovery without limb clipping, torso collapse, scale artifacts or wrong ground contacts |

## 19–23. Stairs, slopes, ladders and climbing

Stairs are a major world-validation requirement: shallow, standard, tall, narrow, wide and spiral stairs where applicable, navigated without constant animation failure. Locomotion distinguishes ordinary steps, elevated steps, step-up obstacles and climbing obstacles, and Durrim legs are never stretched to reach human-authored step heights. Uphill, downhill, side slopes and uneven terrain keep convincing foot placement and alignment. Ladders need Durrim-specific validation (hand and foot rung reach, alternating limbs, torso and equipment clearance), never scaling the ladder or stretching limbs to fit a human animation. Climbing accounts for arm and leg length, hands, feet, torso depth and reach envelope, and any gameplay climbing difference is OPEN.

## 24–25. Legacy swimming and breath traits

"Durrim swim poorly" and "Durrim hold their breath longer than baseline humans" (comparator read as the Marchfolk Human Reference Population; PROJECT_RULES terminology) stay **GAMEPLAY TRAIT — SUBJECT TO LATER REVIEW**, with no biology invented to justify either and breath never derived automatically from torso depth. If swimming is kept, animation and gameplay eventually separate skill, anatomy, equipment burden, stamina and buoyancy; the exact breath multiplier and mechanism stay under gameplay review.

## 26–34. Furniture, world interaction and hands

Sitting is a permanent validation requirement (chairs, benches, stools, ground where supported, thrones, taverns, later mount seating), checking seat height, knee angle, foot and back contact, armrests and torso clearance. Furniture must not assume one humanoid size, but Durrim furniture isn't created everywhere automatically: worldbuilding decides between shared, adjustable or mixed, population-specific and improvised accommodation, and animation handles the approved world. Beds, bedrolls, cots and sleeping surfaces are tested without dynamic prop scaling unless later approved. Tables, counters, bars, desks, workbenches and crafting stations acknowledge height differences, never stretching arms upward to force identical interactions; door handles, latches, push bars, pull handles and large gates use valid reach and contact. Pickups from ground, low shelf, table, counter, chest and wall placement keep hand contact on the actual hand position. **Anatomical arm reach, interaction-system reach, combat reach and camera targeting reach** stay separate, never one global Reach value, with gameplay rules OPEN. Relatively substantial hands are validated with small and large objects, handles, cups, books, coins, tools, weapons, levers and doors, and **anatomy doesn't imply reduced dexterity**: pinch, writing, small-object handling, tool use and fine crafting are validated, and crafting skill is cultural, individual or gameplay, never racial anatomy.

## 35–41. Weapons and shields

> **Canonical weapon dimensions stay independent of the wielder.**

A sword doesn't get shorter, a dagger doesn't get wider and a bow doesn't scale because a Durrim holds it. Grips (one-handed, two-handed, pole weapons, bows, shields, small and large weapons) may need anatomy-aware solutions, never weapon scaling. Weapon dimensions, arm reach, animation path, collision or hit detection and gameplay reach stay separate, short stature is never secretly compensated by scaling weapons, and final combat reach is OPEN. World-standard two-handed weapons are tested for hand spacing, torso and ground clearance, shoulder range, weapon path and stance, assuming neither "dwarf = can't use large weapons" nor that every weapon is equally practical for every anatomy (restrictions OPEN). Polearms are validated for leverage geometry, ground clearance, hand spacing and attack arcs and are never shortened automatically. Bows are validated for bow length, draw length, hand placement, string, chest and arm clearance and Durrim arm span without automatic scaling, and whether some bow dimensions are impractical for some anatomies may become an equipment-design question. A world-standard shield may cover proportionally more of a Durrim body, a real relationship not to normalize away, validated for grip, forearm, ground clearance, visibility and movement.

## 42–53. Armor, clothing and carried equipment

Durrim armor is never **human armor uniformly scaled down**. It accommodates torso breadth and depth, shoulders, neck, pelvis, limb lengths, joint positions, hands, feet and composition, and fitting supports variation in height, frame, muscularity, body fat, limb proportions and torso dimensions, with no single canonical "dwarf body" fit.

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Chest | Independent thoracic breadth, thoracic depth, shoulder breadth, abdomen and composition; no floating armor, clipping or inflated barrel silhouettes |
| Shoulders | Follow actual shoulder joint position, clavicles, neck and arm motion, never scaled human offsets alone |
| Legs | Hip, thigh, knee, lower leg and ankle, with shorter segments never causing pieces to overlap |
| Gloves | Palm breadth and depth, finger lengths and wrists, never uniformly scaled human gloves |
| Footwear | Foot length, breadth, depth, heel, forefoot and ankle; substantial feet stay believable, not oversized fantasy boots |
| Helmets | Cranial anatomy, ear position, hair, facial hair, neck and head movement, never uniformly scaled human helmets |
| Beard with armor | Where facial hair exists, tested with helmet, gorget, chest armor, high collar, ready poses, crouch and head rotation; **beard clipping isn't an acceptable Durrim trait**, and the beard-physics solution is OPEN |
| Clothing | Supports torso depth and breadth, limbs, composition and movement, never stretching textures or patterns |
| Back equipment | Sheathed weapons, bows, quivers, backpacks, cloaks and shields, since torso length and head position may change available back space |
| Belt and hip | Pouches, scabbards, potions, tools and bags, where human offsets may not suit compact torso-pelvis relationships, never shrinking items |

## 54–60. Camera, dialogue, mounts, collision and size consequences

Exploration, combat, dialogue, cinematic and first-person cameras (if used) account for Durrim stature, never positioned as if the character were human height. Dialogue supports meaningful height differences with Durrim, Marchfolk, Skarn, Aelari and, eventually, the full approved range of humanoid statures, including Gorrund once Gorrund anatomy has been defined (a validation reminder only, not a Gorrund specification), without cut-off faces, over-the-head framing or artificial positioning, and eye-lines stay believable across large height differences without stretched necks or forced identical head rotations. Mount compatibility is OPEN; if mounts exist, saddle dimensions, leg and stirrup position, reach, mount and dismount and mounted weapon use are validated, never assuming one saddle fits every race. Collision architecture is OPEN: visual anatomy, gameplay collision and interaction volumes aren't assumed identical, and capsule dimensions aren't finalized. Whether stature affects hurtboxes, hitboxes, targetability, cover or attack avoidance is a gameplay or system decision, never silently normalized or exaggerated during character design.

> **Whether major racial size differences affect collision, reach, movement, combat and related gameplay remains unresolved** (project-wide OPEN); Durrim design keeps meaningful anatomical consequences possible without deciding the model early.

Anatomy-aware contact correction will likely be needed for feet, hands, stairs, slopes, ladders, furniture, weapons and interactions, with the IK solution OPEN.

## 74–81. Carry-forward, adult read, anti-caricature, diversity, presets, randomization and NPCs

## 82–83. Final first-pass Durrim identity

> **Durrim are a distinct compact humanoid population defined by short adult stature, high skeletal structural presence relative to height, a broad/deep and vertically compact torso architecture, substantial joint relationships, coordinated shorter limb contribution, relatively substantial hands and feet, and a distinct compact craniofacial foundation. Their identity persists across frame, composition, facial structure, pigmentation, age, hair and presentation, and does not depend on beard, occupation, culture or caricature.**

Durrim are **not** small Marchfolk, scaled-down Skarn or short humans with beards, and aren't universally muscular, broad-framed, fat, male-coded, pale or ruddy, red-haired or old-looking. They aren't biologically miners or smiths, and they aren't naturally comedic movers.
